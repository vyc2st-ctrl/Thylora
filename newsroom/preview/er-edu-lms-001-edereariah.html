ZONE 2 · LOCAL STORY CORE

A child here was marked wrong on a sum he could do.

The sixth question on his sheet used three ordinary words. He read them the way everyone in this settlement says them. Read that way, the answer he gave was correct.

The evening it happened

ZONE 3 · HUMAN THREAD
  • That dayAn ordinary morning. A sheet of six problems about a mill, a bakehouse and a store room.
  • Five of sixCompleted, and completed correctly. Whatever went wrong on the sixth, it was not arithmetic.
  • The phrase“every other day.”
  • What he took it to meanEvery day. A reading ordinary speech permits. Nothing in the sentence marks it as unusual.
  • At the tableAfter the meal, in the side-light. He did not ask for help. He stopped. The pencil went down and stayed down.
  • What the adult believedThat he had not tried the last one.
  • What changedThe adult asked what the sentence meant instead of what the answer was. He said “every day” — and both of them heard it at once. The arithmetic had never been touched.

The problem, and what turned on three words. The mill sends a load of flour to the bakehouse every other day. Each load is 8 sacks. The bakehouse uses 5 sacks a day. Over 6 days, does the store room go up or down? Read correctly: three loads, 24 sacks in, 30 out — down by six. Read as every day: six loads, 48 in, 30 out — up by eighteen. Same arithmetic. Opposite answers. A phrase containing no numbers decided it.
