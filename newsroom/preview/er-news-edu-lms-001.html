The math test that started before the numbers

A child who cannot read the question has not yet been asked one. We built a three-part gauge to say what a wrong answer actually proves — and, more often, what it does not.

The scene

The paper said twelve. The paper said miles. The paper said flour. It also said approximately, remaining, and at this rate, and by the third of those the boy had stopped reading and started guessing.

He got it wrong. The mark went in the book as a mark about arithmetic.

It was not a mark about arithmetic. Nobody in the room had established that he could not divide. What had been established was that he could not get to the division.

The gauge

VISUAL_LAYER_4 · EVIDENCE
P_solve = L × M × S
LLanguage access Can the learner read and parse the words the problem is written in — including the connective words that carry the logic?
MMathematical operation Can the learner carry out the arithmetic or procedure the problem requires, once it is known?
SSituation model Can the learner map the words onto a structure — who has what, what is changing, what is being asked for?

Each term is scored 0 or 1 for a single problem. P_solve is therefore 1 only when all three hold. Any one at zero makes the product zero.

Three failures that look identical

  • L = 0 — the question did not arrive. The words blocked access. The learner may or may not be able to do the mathematics; this problem cannot tell you.
  • S = 0 — the question arrived and did not resolve. Every word was read and could not be assembled into a situation. A modelling failure — not reading, not arithmetic.
  • M = 0 — the question arrived, resolved, and the procedure failed. The only one of the three that is evidence about mathematics.

What a zero does not prove

P_solve = 0 names a blocked path. It does not name the cause.

When L = 0, M and S are UNASSESSED — not zero, not low, not proven. The problem never reached them. Recording them as failures manufactures a deficiency out of a measurement gap.

The symmetric error is as bad: when L = 0, competence is not proven either. A learner who cannot read the question has not demonstrated that they could have answered it.

Do not infer either competence or deficiency without testing the relevant layer. Test M with the same operation stated in numbers alone. Test S with the same situation stated in the learner's strongest language. Only then is there evidence.

What would change the conclusion

If the same learner, given the same operation stripped of prose, completes it — the original mark was never about mathematics, and every record derived from it is wrong.

If the learner fails the stripped version too, M = 0 is established, and the language question remains separately open rather than answered.

One action each

For the teacherBefore recording a wrong answer on a word problem, re-ask the operation with no prose. One minute. It changes what the mark means.

For the parentAsk your child to tell you what the question wanted, not what the answer was. If they cannot say what it wanted, the arithmetic was never the obstacle.
