## 0 · Truth line (read first)

The Lineage Desk crew are **THYLORA world characters**. They are the faces of the
work inside the world. They are not employees on Earth and no Earth money is paid.

The **research itself is real** and every finding is in the record of evidence
with its source. The **clock is real**: work-log times below come from system
timestamps. Where a time was not captured, the log says so instead of guessing.

The world tells the story of the work. It never invents the evidence.

## 1 · The building

**Sixty Census House** — a four-storey brick building on the corner of Covington
Row and Mason Street, Port District, THYLORA. Old cotton-exchange building, bought
by the Desk and turned inside out: the trading floor is now the reading room.

| Floor | Room | What happens there |
|---|---|---|
| Ground | **The Porch** | Front room. Families walk in, sit, talk. Sweet tea, a voice recorder, a scanner. Nobody fills out a form first. |
| 2 | **The Reading Room** | Long tables, census film readers, the wall map of every county a family passed through. Red string = proven. Pencil = lead. |
| 3 | **The Brick Wall** | One room for 1870 and before. Slave schedules, wills, estate inventories, Freedmen's Bureau files. Quiet room. Tissue box on every table. |
| 4 | **The Writers' Loft** | Where proven lines become stories, comics, songs and film treatments. |
| Roof | **Mirror Deck** | Where crews meet their mirror families (§4). |

Sign over the door: **"We don't guess. We go get it."**

## 4 · Mirror families

Every Earth family the Desk traces gets a **mirror family** in THYLORA: a family
whose road rhymes with theirs but who are their own people, with their own names,
faces, trades and arguments.

**Mirror of Peete · Wright: the Tolliver · Reed family of Hatchie Bend.**
- Their great-great-grandfather **Ambrose Tolliver** was carried west from the
  tobacco coast to the river-bottom cotton country as a boy, the same forced road
  Richard Peete most likely walked.
- His grandson **Solomon Tolliver** married **Vessie Reed** from the Delta side of
  the river (mirror of Willie Elbert and Hattie; mirror of Eugene and Hester).
- Their son **Lucius Tolliver** went north to the iron city of Brickport in the
  1950s, like Walter went to St. Louis.

What the mirror does:
- **Story exchange.** The Peete family tells its stories (Uncle Teddy and Aunt Lois,
  "get cho one-legged self out here"; Chubby's song). The Tollivers tell theirs.
  Families compare notes: what was the same, what was different, and why.
- **Families help each other.** A Tolliver cousin who is a records clerk helps the
  Peete case; the Peete side helps the Tollivers find a lost branch.
- **Rule:** a mirror family never borrows a real person's private details. They
  rhyme with the road, not with the people.

## 5 · Writers' Loft brief (to Ray Bannister — not to the Chairman)

**Working title:** *Sixty Census House: The Road From Mason*
**Form:** a story told by the Desk as they work the case. Each chapter is one
record found. The reader learns how tracing works by watching it done.

**Point of view:** the crew's. The Chairman is the client who walked in off
the Porch. He talks the way he talks (see the Chairman's own words in the
case file: Uncle Teddy at the house after the funeral; "I told told told you
not to mess"). The crew does not polish his voice.

**Chapter plan (one chapter = one record):**
1. **The Porch.** VYC walks in with a name and a funeral memory. Dess opens the board.
2. **The Number.** Pip finds Walter's Social Security file. Mason, 1936. Five brothers.
3. **The Obituary.** John Henry Wright's obituary gives up Eugene and Hester, and Aunt Lois.
4. **Civil District 8.** Marcus reads the 1940 page: a family of seven in Tipton County.
5. **June 4, 1910.** The marriage of Willie Elbert and Hattie Tate.
6. **Booker T.** Why a man born in 1866 named his son after Booker T. Washington.
7. **1870.** Richard and Susan, written down by name for the first time. Junie takes the room upstairs.
8. **The Brick Wall.** Mecklenburg, Virginia. The road west. (Written only as far as the evidence goes. Where the evidence stops, the chapter says so.)
9. **The Mirror Deck.** The Peetes meet the Tollivers.

**Rules for the Loft:**
- Every scene with a fact cites the record in the case file. No invented dates.
- Where the record is silent, the characters say "we don't know yet" out loud.
- Each chapter ends with one **Earth fact** the reader never knew (e.g. the 1870
  census was the first to name formerly enslaved people) and one **THYLORA fact**.
- Math in every chapter: the numbers of the case, done exactly. When a record gives only a year, say so: "the census
  birth years are 1822 and 1866, 44 years apart" — not "he was about 44."

## 7 · Case Card spec (not a generic Earth card)

Front: ancestor name, birth–death, county, and a small map of the road.
Back: **the record that proves it** (type, year, where to find it), **status**
(CONFIRMED / LEAD), **one Earth fact**, **one THYLORA fact**, **the math**
(e.g. "Willie Elbert was 47 years, 7 months, 14 days old when Walter was born: 24 Sep 1888 → 8 May 1936").
A LEAD card prints with a dashed border and cannot be played as proof in the
*Kinfolk Klaim* game until it is CONFIRMED.
