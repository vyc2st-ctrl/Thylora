## D · WHY THIS ONE

Scored against the Chairman's six criteria. Production judgement, not politics.

**1 · Best story potential.** Bramble already has a story that works and that the Chairman
directed personally across five recorded versions. The hesitation beat — a hand that
starts up and stops — is a real dramatic engine. The other three are premises, not
stories: "a city runs out of power", "a family keeps a thing", "leadership changes hands".
Nobody is in them.

**2 · Strongest EdereAirah connection.** This is not close. Bramble carries a locked era
(1930s–1940s), a locked physical appearance, a named second character under a Chairman
name lock, a locked line of dialogue, a locked closing phrase, and a visual law
(`viewer_plane_separation_2026_09_16`) that the **entire company** subsequently inherited
as `THY-VIEWER-PLANE-UNIVERSAL-001`. Every other THYLORA image in existence now follows a
rule that was written for Bramble. The other three titles return zero rows from every
world table queried.

**3 · Best visual potential.** The era lock does the work an art director usually has to
do from nothing: worn wool, chalk dust, slate, kerosene light, oiled floorboards, a
storm lantern, cast-iron, brown paper. The palette is already written down — *earthy
brown, charcoal, worn wool, wood, paper, slate, leather* — against one deep muted red cap.
A city with a power problem has no such inheritance, and a family heirloom story has an
object nobody has designed.

**4 · Clear emotional reason to own it.** A child who has ever not raised their hand
recognises page 12 immediately. The adult reading it aloud recognises it too, which is the
thing that makes a picture book get bought twice.

**5 · Can become the reusable standard.** The Bramble rebuild produces a page grid, a
typographic system, a viewer-plane treatment, a colophon format and a back-matter pattern
that the other three inherit without re-litigation. Building the standard on the title
with the most locked canon means the standard gets tested against real constraints instead
of being designed in the abstract.

**6 · Can justify its price when finished.** At **$1.99** the gap between what exists and
what it needs to be is the smallest of the four. A 28-page illustrated edition at $1.99
is not a defensible price — it is an obviously good one, and it is the right price for a
first title whose job is to make the next purchase easy. The $7.00 Handoff would need to
carry seven times the value against a premise with no characters in it.

**One more reason, which is not a preference.** Bramble has to be rebuilt anyway. Section
K explains why the live listing cannot stay as it is regardless of what the Chairman
decides about quality.

## J · CUSTOMER VALUE TEST

**WHY WOULD SOMEONE WANT THIS?**
Because almost everyone has not raised their hand, and almost nobody has been shown a
picture of the exact second it happens. The book names something common and unnamed. Then
it does the thing that makes a parent buy it: it shows an adult handling it well.

**WHAT WILL THEY REMEMBER?**
The hand that comes up about an inch. And the teacher turning her chair round.

**WHAT WILL THEY LOOK AT AGAIN?**
Spread eleven — chalk dust in low sun. It is the best-lit page and it is deliberately the
reward for finishing. After that, the classroom wide shot on spread two, which is built to
be searched: the child feeding the stove, the feet that do not reach the floor, the empty
desk with a slate still on it, the two inkwell holes with no inkwells.

Make the box. Page 26 gives the dimensions, the eased slot, the string on the pencil and

At $1.99: twenty-eight pages, ten original illustrated plates, a designed interior, a named
imprint, a printed colophon, an author's note, a buildable object and four questions.

that the price is not the problem and never was. The price is defensible the moment the
book is real.

Six things, and they are checkable rather than claimed:
