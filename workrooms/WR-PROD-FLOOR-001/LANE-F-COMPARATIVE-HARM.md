## 1 · Method: the normalization scheme

### 1.1 Harm vector H_i

For each specific product or substance `i` (a row R01–R31), the harm vector is defined as:

```
H_i = ( A_i , C_i , D_i , M_i , K_i , Y_i , X_i )
```

| Symbol | Component | Exact definition used here | Unit | Scale type | Source type | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `i` | row index | one specific product or substance, R01…R31 | none | nominal | this report | — |
| `A_i` | Acute | Worst **acute** outcome documented for a single use at typical exposure, graded 0 = none documented · 1 = transient impairment · 2 = intoxication with injury risk · 3 = medical emergency common · 4 = life-threatening (respiratory depression, coma, death) named in a federal label or agency communication | grade 0–4 | **ordinal** (distances between grades mean nothing) | FDA labels and communications, NIDA | higher = worse |
| `C_i` | Chronic | Chronic organ disease causally linked by a federal agency or IARC at sustained use: 0 none documented · 1 associated only · 2 causal for one organ system · 3 causal for two or more organ systems | grade 0–3 | **ordinal** | CDC, FDA, HHS, IARC | higher = worse |
| `D_i` | Dependence | **Cardinal where measured:** share of past-year users meeting DSM-5 criteria for that substance's use disorder (NSDUH). Otherwise ordinal 0–3 from an FDA label or agency statement | proportion (0–1), or grade | cardinal **or** ordinal, flagged per cell | SAMHSA NSDUH; FDA | higher = worse |
| `M_i` | Mortality | Two separate sub-measures that are **never merged**: `M_i^pop` = deaths per year in the U.S. involving or attributed to `i`; `M_i^user` = `M_i^pop` ÷ exposed persons × 100,000 | deaths/yr; deaths per 100,000 exposed per yr | cardinal (ratio scale) | CDC/NCHS, MMWR | higher = worse |
| `K_i` | Cancer | IARC **strength-of-evidence** group: Group 1 → 3 · 2A → 2 · 2B → 1 · Group 3 or not evaluated → 0. This measures **how sure** science is that the agent causes cancer, **not how much** cancer it causes (IARC's own caveat [V:S13]) | grade 0–3 | **ordinal** | IARC; HHS | higher = stronger evidence |
| `Y_i` | Disability | Years lived with disability, or DALYs, attributable to `i` (GBD) | DALYs/yr | cardinal | IHME GBD (C25) | higher = worse |
| `X_i` | PopulationExposure | Number of U.S. people exposed in the stated window (past month or past year, always labelled) | persons | cardinal | SAMHSA NSDUH; CDC | higher = more exposure (a burden multiplier, **not** a harm on its own) |

**Assumptions:**
1. U.S. national data only.
2. Rows are specific products. Where a data source reports only a class (for example ICD-10 T40.5 covers every form of cocaine), the cell says so and the confidence grade drops.
3. "Involving" counts from death certificates overlap. One death that involves fentanyl and cocaine is counted in both rows [V:S10]. Columns of `M_i^pop` therefore **must not be summed**.

**Failure conditions:**
- The numerator and denominator come from different years or definitions (flagged `B`/`C` below).
- A class-level count is mistaken for a product-level count.
- An ordinal grade is treated as a number that can be added or averaged.
- IARC groups are read as potency.

**Classification of H_i: FORMAL SYSTEM (a measurement construct).** It is not a law of nature. Its components are EMPIRICAL measurements.

### 1.2 Why the endpoints are NOT summed

`A` and `C` are ordinal grades. `D` is either a proportion or a grade. `M` is deaths per year. `K` measures confidence in a hazard, not its size. `X` counts people. Adding "deaths/yr" to "IARC group" to "proportion with a disorder" gives a number with no unit and no meaning. The sum would also change whenever an arbitrary scale is changed, for example if `K` were set to 0–10 instead of 0–3. **Therefore no row gets a single "harm score" in this report.**

### 1.3 How vectors are compared: component-wise Pareto dominance

**Definition (FORMAL SYSTEM LAW, a mathematical definition):**

```
H_i ≽ H_j   ⇔   ∀k ∈ S :  h_{i,k} ≥ h_{j,k}
H_i ≻ H_j   ⇔   H_i ≽ H_j  and  ∃k ∈ S : h_{i,k} > h_{j,k}
H_i ∥ H_j   ⇔   neither H_i ≽ H_j nor H_j ≽ H_i        ("INCOMPARABLE")
```

- `k` is a component index. `S` is the set of components that are **measured with the same definition for both rows** (confidence grade A or B).
- `h_{i,k}` is component `k` of row `i`.
- `≽` means "at least as harmful on every compared component". `≻` means "strictly more harmful" (Pareto dominance). `∥` means incomparable. `∀` = for all. `∃` = there exists. `⇔` = if and only if.
- Unit: unitless (it is a relation, not a number). Domain: pairs of rows. Threshold: none; the result is one of ≻, ≺, ∥ or =.
- **Assumption:** every component points the same way (higher = worse), which holds by construction in §1.1.
- **Failure condition:** if `S` is empty, or holds only components graded C/D, the comparison returns `UNDETERMINED`, not a ranking.

**Confidence grade for each compared component:**
- **A:** both values come from the same fetched primary source with the same definition.
- **B:** fetched primary sources, but the definition or year differs.
- **C:** excerpt-level, or derived with a documented mismatch.
- **D:** unverified.
Only A/B components enter `S`.

**Per-component ranks:** within one component, rows are ranked only when that component is cardinal and graded A/B. Ties and UNKNOWNs stay unranked.

### 1.4 Per-use / per-person risk vs population burden (they are different questions)

**Equation (EMPIRICAL MODEL):**

```
M_i^pop = X_i × r_i          ⇒          r_i = M_i^pop / X_i ,     M_i^user = r_i × 100,000
```

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit | Domain |
|---|---|---|---|
| `M_i^pop` | deaths per year involving or attributed to row `i` | deaths/yr | ≥ 0 |
| `X_i` | exposed persons (window stated) | persons | > 0 |
| `r_i` | average annual death risk per exposed person | deaths per person per yr (unitless per year) | 0–1 |
| `M_i^user` | the same risk per 100,000 exposed | deaths per 100,000 exposed per yr | ≥ 0 |
| `×`, `/` | multiplication, division | — | — |

**Assumptions:**
- Deaths come from the exposed group.
- The window of `X_i` matches the risk period.

**Failure conditions:**
- Mismatched windows (past-month vs past-year).
- Multiple-cause "involving" numerators, which overcount any single drug.
- Long latency: tobacco and alcohol deaths reflect decades of past exposure, not current users.
- Secondhand victims (for example smoking deaths among non-smokers) included in the numerator.

**Threshold:** none. This measures size; it does not trigger any legal consequence.

**Worked example (MATH RULE):**
- Methamphetamine-type stimulants:
  - `M^pop` = 28,722 deaths in 2024 involving "psychostimulants with abuse potential" (T43.6, which also includes amphetamine and methylphenidate) [V:S10].
  - `X` = 2.4 million past-year methamphetamine users, age 12+, 2024 [V:S19].
  - `r` = 28,722 / 2,400,000 = 0.011968 → **`M^user` ≈ 1,197 per 100,000 per year**.
  - Grade **C**, because the numerator class is wider than meth and deaths overlap with fentanyl.
- Alcohol (all beverage classes):
  - `M^pop` = 178,307 deaths per year, averaged over 2020–2021 [V:S14].
  - `X` = 134.3 million past-month drinkers, 2024 [V:S19].
  - `r` = 178,307 / 134,300,000 = 0.0013277 → **`M^user` ≈ 133 per 100,000 per year**.
  - Grade **C**, because the years differ, past-month is not past-year, and the numerator is "attributable" rather than "involving".
- Per-person ratio: 1,197 / 133 ≈ **9.0**. By this measure a person who used meth in the past year had roughly nine times the annual death risk of a past-month drinker.
- Population ratio: 178,307 / 28,722 ≈ **6.2**. By this measure alcohol accounts for roughly six times as many deaths per year.
- Dominance test: on (`M^pop`, `M^user`, `X`) the two rows point in opposite directions. Result: **H_alcohol ∥ H_meth, INCOMPARABLE**. Ranking them requires a **value choice** between protecting the individual user and reducing total deaths, and that choice belongs to lawmakers, not to this measurement.
- Tobacco check:
  - 480,000 deaths per year (includes more than 40,000 non-smoking adults killed by secondhand smoke) [V:S12].
  - `X` = 48.0 million past-month users of any tobacco product [V:S19].
  - `M^user` ≈ 1,000 per 100,000.
  - Grade **C**: the numerator is cigarette-and-secondhand, the denominator is all tobacco products, and the deaths reflect long latency.

**Classification: FORMAL SYSTEM** (a coding of statutes). The `Tax_i` conversions below are arithmetic with one physical constant.

**Worked example 1: the crack/powder quantity ratio (ENGINEERING-style arithmetic on statutory thresholds)**

```
ρ = Q_powder / Q_crack
```

- `ρ` = quantity ratio (unitless).
- `Q` = grams of mixture that trigger the same mandatory minimum.
- Today, 10-year tier: `ρ` = 5,000 g / 280 g = **17.857 ≈ 18:1**. 5-year tier: 500 g / 28 g = 17.857 [V:S02].
- 1986–2010: 5,000 g / 50 g = **100:1**; 500 g / 5 g = 100:1 [V:S17].
- Threshold meaning: at `ρ` = 1 the same weight of either form triggers the same minimum.
- Failure condition: `ρ` compares weights only. Guideline offense levels, charging decisions and relief (safety valve, substantial assistance) change the sentence actually served (see §4.7).

**Worked example 2: federal tax per standard drink (PHYSICAL CONSTANT + arithmetic)**

Physical constant: ethanol density ρ_EtOH ≈ 0.789 g/mL at 20 °C [U:C01, CRC Handbook]. Conversion: 1 U.S. fl oz = 29.5735 mL; 1 U.S. gal = 3,785.41 mL.

- **Standard drink check.** Beer: 12 fl oz × 29.5735 mL/fl oz × 0.05 × 0.789 g/mL = **14.0 g**. Wine: 5 fl oz × 29.5735 × 0.12 × 0.789 = **14.0 g**. Spirits: 1.5 fl oz × 29.5735 × 0.40 × 0.789 = **14.0 g**. All three match NIAAA's 14 g standard [E:E06].
- **Spirits:** $13.50 per proof gallon [E:E08]. One proof gallon = 1 gal at 50 % ABV = 3,785.41 × 0.5 × 0.789 = 1,493.4 g ethanol. Tax per drink = 13.50 × 14 / 1,493.4 = **$0.127**.
- **Beer:** $18 per 31-gal barrel [E:E08]. Ethanol per barrel at 5 % ABV = 31 × 3,785.41 × 0.05 × 0.789 = 4,629.6 g, which is 330.7 drinks. Tax per drink = 18 / 330.7 = **$0.054**.
- **Wine (still, ≤16 %):** $1.07 per gallon [U:C11]. Ethanol per gallon at 12 % ABV = 358.4 g, which is 25.6 drinks. Tax per drink = **$0.042** (UNVERIFIED rate).
- **Cigarettes:** $1.01 per pack of 20 [E:E08], so **$0.0505 per cigarette**.
- **Result:** for the same 14 g of ethanol, federal tax on spirits is about 0.127 / 0.054 ≈ **2.3×** the tax on beer.
- **Assumptions:** general (not small-producer) rates; the ABV values stated above.
- **Failure conditions:** craft or small-producer reduced rates; state taxes, which are excluded here.

## 4 · Analysis: why the legal treatment differs (each explanation attributed)

### 4.1 Legislative record and historical beliefs (crack vs powder)

**What USSC found in 2002:**
- "Cocaine in any form produces the same physiological and psychotropic effects."
- Snorted powder "poses a lesser risk of addiction to the typical user than crack cocaine."
- That difference "independently does not appear to warrant the 100-to-1 drug quantity ratio."
- The 1986 structure "was based on many beliefs about the association of crack cocaine offenses with certain harmful conduct – particularly violence – that are no longer accurate."
- Two-thirds of federal crack offenders were street-level dealers.

[V:S27]

**What Congress did:** in 2010 it cut the ratio to about 18:1 rather than 1:1, and in 2018 it made that change retroactive [V:S17].

**Attribution:** the residual 18:1 is a legislative choice recorded in statute. This report found no fetched source that measures an 18-fold difference in harm.

### 4.2 Medical-use recognition drives schedule, and schedule drives penalty

- The CSA ties Schedule I to "no currently accepted medical use and a high potential for abuse" [V:S21].
- Molecules in the same class therefore split across regimes:
  - Heroin (Schedule I) has quantity-triggered mandatory minimums.
  - Oxycodone, morphine and methadone (Schedule II) sit under (b)(1)(C), and "oxycodone trafficking does not carry a mandatory minimum penalty" [V:S02, V:S09].
  - Pharmaceutical and illicit fentanyl are the same molecule, split by channel [V:S23, E:E11].
- The April 2026 medical-marijuana order goes further: the same plant is Schedule III or Schedule I depending on whether a state medical license covers it [V:S16, E:E05].

### 4.3 Statutory carve-outs for tobacco and alcohol

- **Tobacco:** Congress expressly prohibits FDA from "banning all cigarettes" or "requiring the reduction of nicotine yields … to zero" [V:S25]. Federal policy for the largest measured death burden in this report (>480,000 per year [V:S12]) therefore runs through age limits (21 [E:E02]), labeling, taxes and product standards, not prohibition.
- **Alcohol:** the federal age floor works through highway funds (8 % withheld) [V:S26]. The statutory warning text dates from 27 U.S.C. 215 and does not mention cancer [V:S24], even though HHS finds alcohol causally linked to at least seven cancers [V:S22].

### 4.4 Tax revenue and lawful markets

- Tobacco and alcohol carry federal excise taxes: $1.01 per pack; $13.50 per proof gallon; $18 per barrel of beer [E:E08].
- Illicit markets produce no excise revenue by definition.
- The medical-marijuana order is reported to end Section 280E's disallowance of deductions for licensees going forward [E:E05].
- This report **does not** claim that tax revenue causes lenient treatment. No fetched source establishes that causal link. State: **HOLD_FOR_EVIDENCE**

### 4.6 Prevalence and normalization

The legal products are used by very large shares of the population:
- 134.3 M people drank alcohol in the past month and 48.0 M used tobacco (2024) [V:S19].
- 63 % of adults drink sugar-sweetened beverages daily [E:E17].
- 90 % of Americans eat more sodium than recommended [V:S20].

Scale of exposure is recorded here as a **correlate** of treatment, not a proven cause.

### 4.7 Enforcement practice changes the sentence actually served

- **Charging policy.** The Feb 5, 2025 Attorney General memorandum directs prosecutors to "charge and pursue the most serious, readily provable offense," defined to include "those with the most significant mandatory minimum sentences," and rescinds "any inconsistent previous policy" [V:S18]. Secondary sources state this rescinded the 2022 policy of charging crack like powder [UNVERIFIED_IN_SESSION: check DOJ memo of Dec 16, 2022].
- **Relief rates differ by drug.** Crack offenders received safety-valve relief in 4 % of mandatory-minimum cases vs 39 % for powder. 26 % of crack offenders vs 66 % of powder offenders were in Criminal History Category I [V:S03, V:S04]. Criminal-history profiles therefore mediate part of the gap. The mean sentences are now close (70 vs 68 months). The racial composition differs sharply: 73 % Black for crack; 70 % Hispanic for powder [V:S03, V:S04].
- **Case volume and user volume do not track each other.** Marijuana has the most users (64.2 M) [V:S19] but 2 % of federal trafficking cases [V:S01]. Methamphetamine has 2.4 M users and 47 % of cases [V:S01, V:S19].

### 4.8 Evidence-category mismatch

IARC places processed meat in the same group as tobacco smoking but states that this "does NOT mean that they are all equally dangerous" [V:S13]. Global attributable cancer deaths are about 34,000 for processed meat vs about 1,000,000 for tobacco [V:S13]. Any argument that equates the two products because of the shared group is **rejected by the source itself**.
