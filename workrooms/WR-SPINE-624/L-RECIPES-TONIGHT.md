## 1 · Garlic-Honey Glazed Chicken Thighs with Jasmine Rice (Earth kitchen) — EARTH_PROPOSED

Savory first, slightly sweet: a shiny garlic glaze over seared boneless thighs, served on plain jasmine rice.

| | |
|---|---|
| Yield | 4 servings (about 1½ thighs + ¾ cup cooked rice each) |
| Active time | 35 min |
| Total time | 50 min (includes a 15-min dry-brine that overlaps the rice) |
| Difficulty | Easy to moderate (the only skill is not burning the garlic) |

### Equipment
- 12-in (30 cm) heavy skillet: stainless steel or cast iron (nonstick works but browns less)
- 2–3 qt (2–3 L) heavy saucepan with a tight lid
- Instant-read thermometer (required; see doneness)
- Kitchen scale (recommended), measuring spoons and cups
- Small jar or bowl for the sauce, fine-mesh sieve for rinsing rice, tongs, a plate

### Ingredients

**Chicken**
| Ingredient | US | Metric | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Boneless, skinless chicken thighs | 1½ lb (about 6 thighs) | 680 g | about 4 oz / 113 g each |
| Kosher salt (Diamond Crystal) | ½ tsp | 1.5 g | or ¼ tsp table salt (1.5 g). Morton kosher is denser: use ¼ tsp |
| Black pepper, ground | ¼ tsp | 0.6 g | |
| Neutral high-smoke-point oil (canola, peanut, rice bran) | 1 Tbsp | 15 mL / 14 g | |

**Glaze**
| Ingredient | US | Metric | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Garlic, minced | 8 medium cloves (about 2½ Tbsp minced) | 24 g | the star; do not reduce |
| Fresh ginger, finely grated | 1 tsp | 2 g | optional |
| Soy sauce (regular) | 2 Tbsp | 30 mL / 32 g | provides salt and umami |
| Honey | 2 Tbsp | 30 mL / 42 g | provides sweetness and shine |
| Rice vinegar, unseasoned | 1 Tbsp | 15 mL | provides acid |
| Water or unsalted chicken stock | ¼ cup | 60 mL | |
| Cornstarch | 1 tsp | 3 g | thickens the glaze |
| Unsalted butter | 1 Tbsp | 14 g | |
| Red pepper flakes | ¼ tsp | 0.5 g | optional, for gentle heat |

**Finish:** 2 scallions (30 g), thinly sliced · 1 tsp (3 g) toasted sesame seeds (optional)

**Rice**
| Ingredient | US | Metric |
|---|---|---|
| Jasmine rice | 1½ cups | 280 g |
| Water | 1¾ cups + 1 tsp | 420 g (420 mL) |
| Salt | pinch (optional) | 0.5 g |

**Rice water ratio by weight:** water ÷ rice = 420 g ÷ 280 g = **1.5 : 1**, measured on rice that has been rinsed and
drained. See §8 for the formula.

### Method

1. **Dry-brine the chicken (0:00).** Pat the thighs very dry with paper towels. Season both sides with the salt and
   pepper. Leave them on a plate for 15 min while the rice cooks. You can also refrigerate them uncovered for up to
   24 h. Do not leave raw chicken at room temperature for more than 2 h in total.
2. **Rinse the rice (0:02).** Put the rice in a sieve set in a bowl. Rinse it under cold water, swishing with your
   hand, through 3 changes of water until the water runs mostly clear (about 1 min). Drain for 5 min.
3. **Cook the rice (0:08).** Put the rice and 420 g of water (plus the pinch of salt) in the saucepan. Bring to a
   full boil over high heat, about 5 min. Stir once, cover tightly, and turn the heat to the lowest setting. Cook
   15 min without lifting the lid. Take the pan off the heat and let it stand, covered, for 10 min. Fluff with a fork.
4. **Mix the glaze (0:10).** In the jar, stir or shake together the soy sauce, honey, rice vinegar, water or stock,
   and cornstarch until no lumps remain.
5. **Sear (0:15).** Heat the skillet over medium-high heat for 2 min. Add the oil and heat until it shimmers
   (pan surface about 400 °F / 205 °C if you have an IR thermometer). Lay the thighs in the pan smooth side down.
   **Do not move them for 5–6 min**, until the underside is deep golden-brown and releases easily.
6. **Flip and finish (0:21).** Turn the thighs and cook 4–5 min more, until the thickest part reads
   **175–180 °F (79–82 °C)**. Move them to a plate.
7. **Bloom the garlic (0:27).** Pour off all but about 1 Tbsp (15 mL) of fat. Turn the heat to medium-low and let
   the pan cool for 30 s. Add the butter, garlic, ginger and pepper flakes. Stir for 45–60 s, until fragrant and pale
   gold. **Stop before it browns**: browned garlic tastes bitter.
8. **Glaze (0:29).** Stir the glaze again (the cornstarch settles), then pour it in. It will bubble at once.
   Simmer 1–2 min, scraping up the browned bits, until it coats a spoon. Cornstarch thickens fully only after the
   glaze reaches a boil (about 212 °F / 100 °C).
9. **Coat (0:31).** Put the chicken and its resting juices back in the pan. Turn the thighs in the glaze for 1 min
   over low heat. Check again: every piece must be at least 165 °F (74 °C).
10. **Serve (0:33).** Spoon the rice onto plates and top with the chicken and the extra glaze. Scatter the scallions
    and sesame over the top.

### Doneness: internal temperature
- **Food-safety minimum (USDA FSIS): all poultry to 165 °F (74 °C)**, measured with a food thermometer in the
  thickest part and not touching the pan
  ([FSIS safe temperature chart](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/safe-temperature-chart);
  [FSIS Ask USDA](https://ask.fsis.usda.gov/article/What-is-a-safe-internal-temperature-for-cooking-meat-and-poultry)).
- **Target for thighs: 175–185 °F (79–85 °C).** This is for texture, not safety. Thigh meat has more connective
  tissue and fat than breast. At 165 °F it is safe but can be slightly rubbery near the joint line; at 175–185 °F it
  turns tender and stays juicy. Breast meat, by contrast, dries out above about 165 °F.
- Color and clear juices are **not** reliable safety indicators. Use the thermometer.

### How the sweet-savory balance works
| Taste | Where it comes from | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Salt** | Dry-brine salt and soy sauce | Seasons the meat all the way through; soy supplies most of the salt |
| **Umami** | Soy sauce (free glutamates from fermentation) and the browned fond from the sear (Maillard products) | Gives depth, the "meaty" savoriness |
| **Sweet** | Honey (mostly fructose and glucose) | Makes the glaze shine and balances the salt. Honey:soy is about **1.3 : 1 by mass** (42 g : 32 g), which reads as *slightly* sweet. For a sweeter glaze go to 3 Tbsp honey; for more savory, 1½ Tbsp |
| **Acid** | Rice vinegar | Cuts the honey, so the glaze tastes bright instead of cloying |
| **Aromatic / heat** | Garlic, ginger, optional chili | The lead flavor; bloomed gently in butter so it tastes sweet-pungent, not bitter |
| **Fat** | Butter plus rendered chicken fat | Carries the garlic aroma and gives the glaze body |

If it tastes flat, add a few drops of vinegar. If it tastes harsh or salty, add ½ tsp honey. If it tastes cloying,
add a splash of soy sauce.

### Substitutions
- **Gluten-free:** use tamari labeled gluten-free instead of soy sauce (most soy sauce contains wheat).
- **Lower sodium / soy-free:** use coconut aminos (2 Tbsp). It is sweeter, so cut the honey to 1 Tbsp.
- **Honey:** use maple syrup (2 Tbsp / 40 g) or packed brown sugar (2 Tbsp / 27 g) plus 1 Tbsp water.
- **Rice vinegar:** use apple cider vinegar (use a little less) or fresh lime juice.
- **Butter / dairy-free:** use 1 Tbsp more oil.
- **Cornstarch:** use arrowroot (1 tsp). Add it at the end and do not boil hard.
- **Bone-in, skin-on thighs (about 2 lb / 900 g):** sear skin side down 6–8 min, then roast at 425 °F (220 °C) for
  20–25 min to 180–185 °F (82–85 °C). Make the glaze in the skillet afterward.
- **Chicken breast:** cook to 165 °F (74 °C) and no higher; slice before glazing.
- **Brown rice:** 280 g rice to 530 g water (about 1.9 : 1 by weight); simmer 40–45 min, then rest 10 min.
- **Rice cooker:** use the cooker's own markings for rinsed jasmine rice.

### Allergens
Contains **soy** and **wheat** (soy sauce), **milk** (butter), and **sesame** if the seeds are used. Sesame has been
the ninth major US food allergen since 2023. It can be made free of wheat, milk and sesame with the substitutions
above. General food-safety label: honey should not be given to infants under 1 year.

### Storage, reheating and food safety
- **2-hour rule:** refrigerate leftovers within **2 hours** of cooking, or within **1 hour** if the room or outdoor
  temperature is above 90 °F (32 °C)
  ([FSIS Leftovers and Food Safety](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/food-safety/safe-food-handling-and-preparation/food-safety-basics/leftovers-and-food-safety)).
- Cool the rice quickly: spread it in a shallow container no more than 2 in (5 cm) deep. Cooked rice left warm can
  grow *Bacillus cereus*, whose toxin survives reheating.
- Refrigerator at **40 °F (4 °C) or below**: eat within **3–4 days**. Freezer at 0 °F (−18 °C): best quality within
  2–3 months.
- Reheat to **165 °F (74 °C)**. Add 1 Tbsp water to the rice and cover it so it steams.

## 2 · Historical Earth Chinese fried rice: evidence verdict and modern recipe

**Bottom line:** the Sui story is a legend attached to a dish *name*. The Qing Yi Bingshou attribution rests on late
secondary sources. The only specification that can be documented is the **modern** Yangzhou standard (2002, revised
2015). So, as the brief instructs, what follows is a modern recipe, **not a historical one**.

### 2.2 Yangzhou-Style Fried Rice: modern, not historical (EARTH_PROPOSED home adaptation of the 2015 standard's ingredient list)

The **ingredient identities**, the **3–4 eggs**, and the sensory targets come from the 2015 standard as reported.
The heritage description supplies the technique cues: steamed "backbone" rice (身骨), eggs set to "osmanthus" curds,
adding the rice when the egg is half-set (击其半渡), and scallion added in three stages
([Baidu Baike](https://baike.baidu.com/item/%E6%89%AC%E5%B7%9E%E7%82%92%E9%A5%AD%E5%88%B6%E4%BD%9C%E6%8A%80%E8%89%BA/58368945)).
**Every quantity other than the eggs, and every home-kitchen step, is INFERRED.** The standard's gram weights were not
obtainable. Because "扬州炒饭" is a registered collective trademark, this is sold (if ever) as "Yangzhou-style",
never as certified.

Yield 2–3 servings · active 40 min (after soaking) · 14-in (35 cm) carbon-steel wok or 12-in skillet · home burner at
maximum.

| Ingredient | US | Metric | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cooked long-grain indica rice, steamed, cooled and loosened (from 1 cup / 185 g raw) | 3 cups | 500 g | ingredient: standard; amount: INFERRED |
| Eggs, large | 3 | 150 g | standard (3–4) |
| Cooked chicken thigh, ¼-in (6 mm) dice | ⅓ cup | 50 g | standard; amount INFERRED |
| Chinese cured ham (Jinhua-type) or smoked ham, diced | ¼ cup | 30 g | standard; amount INFERRED |
| Shrimp, peeled, diced, coated in ½ tsp cornstarch + a little egg white | ⅓ cup | 60 g | standard ("上浆"); amount INFERRED |
| Dried scallop, soaked 2 h then shredded | 2 pieces | 10 g dry | standard; amount INFERRED |
| Dried shiitake/flower mushroom, soaked 30 min then diced | 2 caps | 6 g dry | standard; amount INFERRED |
| Bamboo shoot, blanched, diced | ¼ cup | 30 g | standard; amount INFERRED |
| Green peas, blanched | ¼ cup | 35 g | standard; amount INFERRED |
| Rehydrated sea cucumber, diced | optional | 30 g | standard; **optional for home: INFERRED** |
| Scallion, thin sliced, in 3 portions | 3 stalks | 45 g | heritage description (three additions) |
| Neutral oil | 3 Tbsp | 45 mL | INFERRED |
| Salt | ½ tsp | 3 g | INFERRED (standard: "salty-savory") |
| Chicken stock (optional; to moisten the dice) | 2 Tbsp | 30 mL | INFERRED |

Method (all steps **INFERRED** except where tagged):
1. Rice: soak, then steam (heritage description: "soak, blanch, steam"). Home version: cook rice at 1.3 : 1 water
   by weight, spread it on a tray, and refrigerate uncovered for 2–12 h so the grains firm up. Break up the clumps by
   hand.
2. Blanch the peas and bamboo shoot for 1 min. Stir-fry the coated shrimp in 1 Tbsp oil until just opaque (about
   1 min) and remove. Briefly toss the ham, chicken, mushroom, scallop and sea cucumber with 2 Tbsp stock over medium
   heat, then remove.
3. Heat the wok until smoking. Add 2 Tbsp oil and the first third of the scallion (fragrance for the rice). Add the
   beaten eggs and push them into curds. **When the egg is half-set, add the rice** (heritage description: 击其半渡).
   Toss 2–3 min until every grain is coated and separate, adding the second third of the scallion (for the egg).
4. Add all the diced ingredients, the salt, and the last third of the scallion (for the dice). Toss 1–2 min until
   everything is steaming hot throughout: **165 °F (74 °C)**, because the dice were pre-cooked and the rice is a
   leftover.
5. Check against the standard's targets: grains separate and glossy; red, green, yellow, white and orange all
   visible; salty-savory, not greasy.

Allergens: egg, crustacean shellfish (shrimp), molluscan shellfish (scallop), fish-adjacent (sea cucumber is an
echinoderm; flag it for shellfish-avoiding guests), soy if soy sauce is added (the standard lists none). Storage:
same as §1.

### 3.3 Method: Earth-unit adapter for testing (EDEREAIRAH_PROPOSED)
Serves 4 · fowl: 4 bone-in thighs and legs, about 2 lb (900 g) · grain: 1½ cups (280 g) · rich stock: 2 cups (475 mL)
· garlic: 1 head (40 g), crushed · butter: 2 Tbsp (28 g) · honey-like sweetener: 2 Tbsp (42 g) · verjuice-like sour:
2 Tbsp (30 mL) · salt: 1 tsp (about 5 g) in total · rosemary: 1 sprig (from THY-PLANT-ROSEMARY-0001).
1. At first bank-up, salt the joints and rub them with half the crushed garlic and rosemary leaves. Let them rest on a
   cool slate.
2. Bring the stock to the boil on the crane over the wood fire. Add the grain and a pinch of salt, lid it, and set the
   pot into the banked embers. It absorbs over about 25–35 min *(Earth-adapter estimate; period timing is by
   observation)*.
3. Rake a charcoal stove-hole to a bright, steady glow. Sear the joints skin side down on the gridiron until
   well-colored, turning them to cook through. Earth estimate: 25–30 min total; Earth safety target: 165 °F / 74 °C
   at minimum, and thighs are better at 180–185 °F / 82–85 °C.
4. Move the tinned-copper pan to a banked hole. Melt the butter, add the rest of the garlic, and stir until fragrant.
   Add the sweetener and the sour, then a ladle of stock from the grain pot. Reduce to a glaze.
5. Turn the joints in the glaze. Spoon the grain into a warmed dish and lay the fowl on top with the glaze over it.

**Why she makes it this way (summary):** the kitchen's heat is layered: wood for volume, embers for holding, charcoal
for precision. Her dish uses each layer for the job it does best, and the most scorch-prone step (garlic) goes on the
gentlest heat.

## 4 · Banana-Walnut Bread: clean, simple, conventional Earth oven (EARTH_PROPOSED)

| Yield | One 9×5 in (23×13 cm) loaf, 10 slices |

### Equipment

### Ingredients
| Ingredient | US | Metric | Why it's there |

| Vanilla extract | 1 tsp | 5 mL | Aroma that rounds out the banana |
| All-purpose flour (spooned and leveled) | 1½ cups | 190 g | The structure: starch plus gluten |
| Baking soda | 1 tsp | 4.6 g | Leavening (reacts with banana and brown-sugar acids); raises pH for better browning |
| Fine salt | ¼ tsp (or ½ tsp Diamond kosher) | 1.5 g | Sharpens the flavor and balances the sweetness |
| Ground cinnamon | ½ tsp | 1 g | Optional warmth |
| Walnuts, toasted, chopped | ¾ cup | 85 g | Crunch plus a gently bitter, tannic contrast to the sweet crumb; toasting deepens flavor |

### Method
1. **Heat and prep.** Rack in the center; heat the oven to 350 °F (175 °C). Grease the pan and line it with a
   parchment sling overhanging the long sides.
2. **Toast the walnuts.** Spread them on the sheet pan and toast 8–10 min until fragrant and a shade darker. Cool and
   chop coarsely. Keep 2 Tbsp (15 g) for the top.
3. **Mash.** Mash the bananas in the large bowl, leaving a few pea-sized lumps.
4. **Wet mix.** Whisk in the melted butter (warm, not hot), brown sugar, eggs and vanilla until smooth.
5. **Dry mix.** In the second bowl, whisk the flour, baking soda, salt and cinnamon so the soda is evenly spread
   (pockets of soda taste soapy).
6. **Combine.** Fold the dry mix into the wet with the spatula until **no dry streaks remain, about 15 folds; stop
   there**. Overmixing develops gluten, which makes a tough, tunneled loaf. Fold in the walnuts.
7. **Pan.** Scrape the batter into the pan, level it, and scatter the reserved walnuts on top.
8. **Bake 55–65 min.** Start checking at 50 min. If the top is dark brown by 40 min, lay a loose sheet of foil over it.
9. **Doneness (any two of these):**
   - Center internal temperature **200–205 °F (93–96 °C)**
   - A skewer in the center comes out with a few moist crumbs but **no wet batter**
   - The top springs back when pressed and the crack looks dry
10. **Cool.** 10 min in the pan, then lift it out with the sling onto the rack. Cool **at least 1 h** before slicing;
    the crumb finishes setting as steam escapes.

### Banana ripeness guide
| Peel | Use for bread? | What's happening |
|---|---|---|
| Green-tinged yellow | No | Mostly starch; bland, firm, dry loaf |
| Full yellow, few specks | Acceptable | Moderate sugar; milder loaf |
| **Heavily brown-speckled** | **Ideal** | Much of the starch has turned to sugar; aromatic, easy to mash |
| Mostly black, very soft | Very good | Sweetest and most aromatic, provided there is no mold, no leaking, and no alcohol or vinegar smell |
| Quick-ripen trick | Fallback | Bake unpeeled bananas at 300 °F (150 °C) for 15–20 min until black. This softens them and concentrates the flavor a little, but adds far less sugar than natural ripening |

### Variations
- **No refined sugar:** replace the brown sugar with ⅓ cup (105 g) pure maple syrup or (112 g) honey. Lower the oven
  to 325 °F (165 °C), bake 60–70 min, and tent with foil by 35 min (liquid sweeteners brown faster). Or omit the
  sweetener entirely and use 400 g of very black bananas: the loaf is less sweet and denser.
- **Oil instead of butter:** ⅓ cup (67 g) neutral oil. The crumb stays softer in the refrigerator.
- **Whole grain:** replace half the flour with white whole-wheat flour (95 g) and add 1 Tbsp milk or water.
- **Nut-free:** omit the walnuts, or use ¾ cup (60 g) rolled oats or chocolate chips. Avoid sunflower seeds: they can
  turn harmlessly green with baking soda.
- **Muffins:** 12 cups at 350 °F (175 °C) for 20–24 min, to the same 200–205 °F.

### Allergens
Contains **tree nuts (walnuts)**, **wheat**, **egg** and **milk**. Walnut is a major tree-nut allergen: for allergic
guests, use the nut-free variation and bake in equipment that has been cleaned of nut residue.

### Altitude note
Above about **3,000 ft (915 m)**:
- Raise the oven **15–25 °F (8–14 °C)**.
- Reduce the baking soda: King Arthur's chart starts at 1½ tsp, so extending it proportionally to 1 tsp gives about
  **¾ tsp at 3,000–5,000 ft and ½ tsp at 5,000–8,000 ft**. This extension is **INFERRED**.
- Consider 1 Tbsp less sugar and 1–2 Tbsp more liquid, and check for doneness 5–8 min early.

Sources: [King Arthur High-Altitude Baking](https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/learn/resources/high-altitude-baking);
[CSU Extension High Elevation Food Preparation](https://extension.colostate.edu/resource/high-elevation-food-preparation-guide/).

### Storage
Wrapped at room temperature 2–3 days; refrigerated up to 1 week; frozen, sliced and wrapped, up to 3 months.

## 8 · Math (Math Display Law, BRIEF §4.4)

### 8.1 Recipe scaling factor
**k = S_t ÷ S_b** and, for each ingredient i, **q_t,i = k × q_b,i**

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Symbols | k = scaling factor; S_t = target servings (subscript t = target); S_b = base servings (subscript b = base); q_b,i = base quantity of ingredient i; q_t,i = scaled quantity of ingredient i |
| Operators | ÷ (division), × (multiplication) |
| Units | k: **unitless** (servings ÷ servings). q keeps the ingredient's own unit (g, mL, count) |
| Domain | S_t > 0, S_b > 0; k > 0 |
| Scale | Reliable for about 0.5 ≤ k ≤ 2; use caution outside that range |
| Threshold | Outside 0.5–2, re-test. For eggs, round to whole or beaten half-eggs (about 25 g each half) |
| Assumptions | Ingredient roles scale linearly; same pan geometry per portion; same heat source |
| Failure condition | **Does not scale linearly:** cooking and baking times (these depend on thickness and pan size, not quantity); leavening at altitude; salt in reductions (evaporation concentrates it); pan size (k = 2 needs 2 pans or a pan with about 2× the area, not a deeper pan); searing (a crowded pan steams instead of browning) |
| Plain English | Divide how many people you're feeding by how many the recipe feeds, then multiply every ingredient by that number |
| Child-readable | "If the recipe feeds 4 and you need 2, that's half. Use half of everything." |
| Worked example | §1 feeds 4; you need 6. k = 6 ÷ 4 = 1.5. Chicken 680 g × 1.5 = 1,020 g (2¼ lb). Honey 42 g × 1.5 = 63 g (3 Tbsp). Sear in 2 batches (failure condition: crowding) |
| Class | Definition of k: **FORMAL_SYSTEM_LAW** (a definition). Applying q_t = k × q_b to real cooking: **HEURISTIC** |

### 8.2 Rice water ratio (by weight)
**r = m_w ÷ m_r**

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Symbols | r = water-to-rice ratio; m_w = mass of cooking water (subscript w = water); m_r = mass of dry rice (subscript r = rice) |
| Units | **unitless** (g ÷ g) |
| Domain / scale | r > 0. Typical ranges: rinsed jasmine, lidded stovetop **1.4–1.6**; fried-rice rice **1.2–1.35**; brown rice **1.8–2.0** |
| Threshold | Below about 1.2: crunchy centers. Above about 1.8 for white rice: mushy |
| Assumptions | Tight lid, low simmer, rice rinsed and drained 5 min (the rice carries some water), sea-level boiling |
| Failure condition | Loose lid or wide pan (more evaporation → raise r); high altitude (lower boiling point → longer cook and slightly higher r); a different variety or an aged crop |
| Plain English | Weigh the rice, then use 1.5 times that weight of water for fluffy jasmine |
| Child-readable | "For every 2 spoons of rice, use 3 spoons of water." |
| Worked example | m_r = 280 g, r = 1.5 → m_w = 1.5 × 280 g = 420 g |
| Class | **EMPIRICAL_MODEL** |
