Before You Buy

Ten minutes, eight fields, one purchase. Not to talk you out of spending money — to make sure that what you are paying for is the thing you were shown.

Most money is not lost to outright lies. It is lost in the gap between a claim and the evidence for it, and in the difference between a price and a total cost. This instrument works that gap.

FIVE CONFUSIONS THAT COST MONEY

Each one is two different things wearing the same coat

You do not need to be suspicious of everybody. You need to keep five pairs apart. Nearly every regretted purchase collapsed one of these pairs into a single idea.

Claim ≠ EvidenceA claim is a sentence. Evidence is what would let someone else check it. "Cuts your bill by 40%" is a claim until somebody can say measured how, over what period, against what.
Price ≠ Total costThe price is the number on the button. The total cost includes delivery, installation, the thing it needs that you do not own, the subscription after month three, and what it costs to stop.
Review ≠ Independent testA review says what one person felt. A test says what happened under stated conditions that somebody else could repeat. Volume of reviews is not strength of evidence.
Access ≠ AuthorityBeing able to reach you is not the same as being entitled to act. An email, a call, a badge, a familiar logo — none of it establishes that this person may ask you for what they are asking for.
Unknown ≠ False"I cannot verify this" is not "this is a scam". It is a reason to hold, ask, or buy smaller — not a verdict on anybody's honesty.

THE TEN-MINUTE RULE

Ten minutes is the budget. If the eight fields on the next plates can all be filled in inside ten minutes, you have enough to decide. If they cannot, that is your finding: the thing being sold is harder to check than it is being made to look.

FIELD ONE & TWO

Question & Claim

QUESTION

What are you actually deciding? Not "is this good" — that has no answer. Something like "do I buy this, at this price, this week, for this job?"

The purchase decision, in one sentence
The job you need it to do The real one. Not the category.
When you need to decide by, and why then If the deadline came from the seller, write that down here.

CLAIM

Write the seller's main promise in their words, exactly. Copying it is the work: vague claims usually stop sounding convincing the moment they are written on a line by themselves.

The main claim, word for word
Now rewrite it as something checkable Turn "saves you money" into "reduces X by Y over Z, compared with W". If you cannot, the claim is not checkable — and that is your first finding.
PLAIN

What exactly are they promising me?

EVERYDAY

If this worked exactly as advertised, what would be different in my house, my week, or my bank account — and by how much?

TECHNICAL

Restate the claim with its measure, its magnitude, its period, and its baseline. A claim missing any of the four cannot be tested and cannot be enforced later.

FIELD THREE & FOUR

Source & Evidence

SOURCE — WHO IS SAYING IT

Who makes this claim, and what do they get if you believe it Seller, manufacturer, affiliate, reviewer, a person in a video, a friend who was also sold it.
Can you reach them another way? A registered business name, a physical address, a phone number that answers, a returns address.
Who is standing behind it if it fails? The seller, a manufacturer warranty, a platform's protection, your card issuer, or nobody.

ACCESS IS NOT AUTHORITY

Someone contacting you first — by call, message, email or knock — has demonstrated only that they can reach you. If any part of this involves an unexpected contact, stop and reach the organisation through a number or address you found yourself. Nothing legitimate is lost by doing so.

EVIDENCE — WHAT WOULD SHOW IT IS TRUE

Tick what actually exists. Not what is implied, not what a badge suggests — what you could open and read in the next ten minutes.

What the evidence actually says — and where you found it

READING REVIEWS WITHOUT BEING FOOLED BY THEM

Ignore the average and read the detailed middle. A review that names a specific condition — how long they had it, what they compared it with, what went wrong — is worth fifty that say "great product". Uniform enthusiasm is weaker evidence than a mixed picture with detail in it.

The price is one line of this. Fill every line you can, and write “unknown” on any you cannot — an unknown line is a finding, not a blank.

Total cost over the first twelve months, as best you can tell Write the number and then write which lines are guesses.

THREE PLACES THE REAL NUMBER USUALLY HIDES

After the introductory period. A price that rises in month four is the price. Multiply by twelve, not by three.

In the exit. What it costs to stop is part of what it costs to start. If leaving is expensive or unclear, the thing is dearer than it looks.

In the thing it needs. The accessory, the app tier, the cable, the annual service, the compatible model of something you already own but not the right version.

FIELD SIX & SEVEN

Constraint & Unknown

CONSTRAINT — WHAT IS ACTUALLY LIMITING YOU

Every purchase runs into a limit: money, space, time, compatibility, somebody else's agreement, or a rule you are inside. Naming the binding one usually decides the purchase faster than any amount of comparison.

What limits this decision — and which limit binds first
What this money is otherwise for If it has another job already, say what.
Who else has to agree, or is affected

UNKNOWN — WHAT YOU CANNOT ESTABLISH IN TEN MINUTES

What you could not check List it plainly. Then mark each one: would this change my answer if it went the wrong way?
Of those, which could you find out — and how long would it take? One message to the seller. One phone call. One night's wait. Often that is the whole gap.

UNKNOWN IS NOT FALSE — AND NOT TRUE EITHER

Failing to verify something is not evidence that somebody is dishonest. Plenty of good sellers document badly. The finding is about your position, not their character: you would be spending on a claim you cannot currently check.

That is sometimes fine — for a small amount, from somewhere you can get a refund. It is not fine for an amount you would miss, or where undoing it is hard. Match the size of the unknown to the size of what you are risking.

PRESSURE IS A FINDING, NOT A REASON

A deadline, a countdown, a last-unit warning, or "I can only hold this price today" is information about the seller's process — write it on the constraint line. A genuine offer survives a night's sleep. If the offer cannot survive ten minutes of checking, the ten minutes has already told you what you needed.

FIELD EIGHT

Act · Ask · Hold

Three endings. Pick one, write why, and put a date on it.

A

Act

The claim is checkable, the evidence exists, the total cost is known and acceptable, and nothing you could not verify would change the answer. Buy it — and keep the receipt, the terms and the return window where you can find them.

B

Ask

One specific thing is missing and one specific person could supply it. Ask for it in writing. How they answer a plain, answerable question is itself evidence — often better evidence than the answer.

C

Hold

An unknown would change your answer, or the total cost is not knowable yet. Hold on purpose: name the fact that would release it and the date you will have it. Holding is a decision. Drifting is not.

My decision, and the reason in one sentence
If ASK — the exact question, and to whom
If HOLD — the releasing fact, and the date

WORKED EXAMPLE

A window film that claims to cut heating costs

This example is invented. The seller, the product and the numbers below are fictional, written to show the instrument working. It is not a review of anything you can buy.

What was seen

An advertisement for “ThermaLayer” window film from a fictional seller, Kesslow Home. Headline: “Cuts heating costs by up to 40%. Fits any window in minutes.” Price shown: $89 for a kit. Four-and-a-half stars from 2,300 reviews. A countdown offering 30% off “today only”.

QuestionDo I buy one $89 kit this week to make the back room usable in winter?
Claim, their words"Cuts heating costs by up to 40%. Fits any window in minutes."
Claim, made checkableAttempted: reduces heating energy use by 40% — measured how, over what period, against what baseline, in what kind of window? None of the four is stated. "Up to" sets no floor: 2% satisfies the claim.
SourceThe seller, who is paid if I believe it. A business name and a returns address are published. No manufacturer named separately.
EvidenceNo stated test conditions. No result from anyone without a stake. Reviews are numerous but nearly all one line. One detailed three-star review says it helped a draught but changed no bill — and it is not answered. A 30-day guarantee exists; the buyer pays return postage.
Total cost$89 kit · $9 delivery · needs a $14 applicator not in the kit · two evenings of my time · $12 return postage if it fails. First-year cost if kept: about $112. Cost if returned: about $21 and two evenings.
ConstraintMoney is available. The binding limit is that the back room has one large old window and the kit is sized for standard panes — fit is uncertain.
UnknownWhether 40% is achievable in any real room. Whether it fits this window. The first is not findable in ten minutes. The second is — measure the window and read the kit's stated size.

WHAT THE CHECK RETURNED

Act — but on a corrected claim. The 40% figure is not evidence and was set aside entirely; it has no measure, no period and no baseline, and “up to” makes it unfalsifiable. What survived was smaller and real: one detailed review reports a reduced draught, and the exposure is about $21 if it fails.

So: measure the window first (two minutes, and it settles the only findable unknown), then buy one kit for the draught — not for the bill. The countdown was written on the constraint line as a fact about the seller and otherwise ignored. The return date went straight into the calendar.

Notice the instrument did not say no. It separated a claim that could not be checked from a benefit that could, and re-sized the purchase to match the evidence that actually existed. A regretted purchase here would not have been $89 — it would have been $89 spent expecting a 40% bill reduction that was never promised in any checkable form.
