## The problem it addresses

Going round the same thing. Not procrastination and not indecision — the
specific state where you keep arriving back at a point you have already been to,
having spent real effort getting there.

## Mechanism — locked

A loop runs on **fuel**. Remove the fuel and the lap stops; attack the lap
itself and it restarts tomorrow.

| Part | What the reader does | Why it is there |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Loop trace** | Write the **last three laps** in order. Mark the move that appears in all three. | A loop is *confirmed*, not assumed. Three laps is the cheapest confirmation that also shows the shape. |
| **2 · Fuel** | Identify which of four fuels keeps the lap running. | Every lap is powered by something unfinished. Naming it converts the loop into a task. |
| **3 · Cut point** | Find the **thinnest** point in the lap, not the most painful one. | People cut where it hurts, which is usually the most defended point and the most expensive to break. |
| **4 · Re-entry rule** | Write what would make it legitimate to think about this again, and when. | Without it, the loop resumes the moment the discomfort returns. The rule is what makes a cut hold. |

### The four fuels

| Fuel | Sounds like | What ends it |
|---|---|---|
| **An unanswered question** | "I still don't know whether…" | Get the answer, or declare it unanswerable and decide anyway. |
| **An unmade decision** | "I keep weighing it up." | The decision. A QuickCheck sheet is the intended companion here. |
| **A missing person** | "I need to talk to them first." | One conversation, scheduled with a date. |
| **An unaccepted cost** | "There's no good option." | Naming the cost out loud and accepting that you are paying it. |

### Readout — four states

| State | Fires when | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| **Cut here** | The lap is confirmed and the thinnest point is identified. | Break it at that point and write the re-entry rule. |
| **Answer the question** | The fuel is an unanswered question. | It is not a loop, it is a lookup you keep deferring. |
| **Make the decision** | The fuel is an unmade decision. | Stop resetting and run a QuickCheck on it. |
| **This is not a loop** | The three laps differ, or each lap made progress. | **It is an unfinished job that feels like a loop because it is long.** The reset does not apply, and saying so is the honest output. |

That fourth state is the integrity of the product. An instrument that never says
"not applicable" is a horoscope.

fuel is an *unaccepted cost* rather than a missing fact, which is the fuel
readers most often misdiagnose as a missing fact — and it demonstrates the
hand-off to QuickCheck without the two products duplicating each other.
