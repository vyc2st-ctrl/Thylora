THYLORA · QYRIS QUICKCHECK How to run it

How to run it

A QuickCheck is not a review meeting and not a quality process. It is nine questions asked once, in order, against one specific thing, by whoever is about to be responsible for it.

1 · Name the subject in one line

Not "the product". "The holiday pre-order page going live Friday." If you cannot name it in one line, you are checking more than one thing, and the verdict will be useless. Split it and run two checks.

2 · Run all nine, in order, without stopping to fix

Finding is one motion; fixing is another. Mixing them means the last three inspections never get run, which is where money and rights usually sit. Write what you find, move on, fix afterwards.

3 · Give every inspection one of three dispositions

Routed

Found and handled, now, by you. Write what you did — not "fixed".

Held

Real, and needs an authority you do not have. Name the person and the date.

Open

Found and not handled. Allowed to exist. Not allowed to be silent.

A blank row is not a fourth disposition. A blank row means the inspection was not run, and the check is not finished.

4 · Take the verdict the rows give you

Release All nine run. Nothing open. Every held item has a name and a date against it.
Route first Something is open and has a safe, reversible route. Take the route, then release.
Hold An open item touches money, rights, safety or a person's name, and has no route yet.

The verdict is read off the rows. It is not a mood, a vote, or a negotiation with the deadline.

The nine inspections

Ask the question out loud. Write the answer, not the intention.

01

Missing prerequisite

What has to be true before this, that is not true yet?

Passes when you can list every prerequisite and point at where each one was satisfied. Fails as "we'll sort that out after launch".

RouteList them all in one go, not one per day. A person told about one blocker at a time will be told about six blockers over six days.
02

Hidden handoff

Where does this pass from one person or system to another with no record of who moved it?

Passes when every handoff leaves a trace somebody else can read later. Fails as "I sent it to them" with no when, no what, and no reply.

RoutePut the trace where the work already is — the thread, the order, the file — not in a separate log nobody opens.
03

Authority mismatch

Is anyone being asked to approve something they cannot actually decide — or able to do something they were never meant to?

Passes when the person deciding owns the consequence. Fails as access mistaken for permission: someone can, so it is assumed they may.

RouteName the decider by name for each decision. "The team" is not a decider.
04

Evidence gap

Which claim here is being asserted with nothing behind it?

Passes when every number and every "done" has a source you could show a stranger. Fails as a figure that came from a conversation and is now in a document.

RouteKeep the claim and label it: modelled, quoted, or measured. A labelled estimate is usable. An unlabelled one becomes a fact by Thursday.
05

Unnecessary waiting

What is being held that could move right now?

Passes when everything not blocked is already moving. Fails as a whole piece of work parked behind one decision it only partly needs.

RouteSplit the blocked part from the rest and release the rest. Waiting is a choice, and it should be a named one.

06

Friction on the other side

Where does the person receiving this hit a wall with no reason and no way forward?

Passes when every refusal states the reason and the next step. Fails as "not eligible", "something went wrong", or silence after payment.

RouteRewrite every dead end as reason + route. Two sentences. This is the cheapest inspection to fix and the most expensive to skip.
07

Rights and privacy

Whose name, face, words, health, money or property is in this — and did they agree to this particular use?

Passes when you can name the basis for each one: owned, licensed, consented, or public with permission. Fails as "it was already on our system".

RouteCollect less. A detail you never hold cannot leak, cannot be subpoenaed, and cannot be wrong about someone.
08

Value left on the table

What is already built, paid for or written here that is not earning or being used?

Passes when each asset is either in use, deliberately shelved, or retired. Fails as finished work sitting in a folder because nobody owns releasing it.

RouteOne line per asset: in use, shelved until when, or retired. Shelved with no date is the same as lost.
09

Failure and recovery

When this fails — not if — what happens to the money, the work and the record?

Passes when a failure holds rather than deletes, and somebody is told. Fails as a retry that charges twice, or a new version that overwrites the old one.

RouteMake failure hold the value and name the state: held, and here is why. Nobody minds waiting nearly as much as they mind silence.

If you only ever run three

Run 04 evidence, 07 rights and 09 failure. Those three are where the damage is permanent. The other six cost you time; these three cost you money, trust, or somebody else's name.

Worked example

Halden Street Bakery — a fictional six-person bakery — is two days from opening holiday box pre-orders on its website. One person runs the QuickCheck.

Subject, in one line: The holiday box pre-order page going live Friday at 9am.

Run by: M. Adeyemi, who will be responsible for it · Date: 14 November · Time taken: 11 minutes

#What was foundDisposition What was done, or who now owns it
01 Boxes are priced but the courier cut-off date was never confirmed, so the page would promise delivery the bakery cannot guarantee. Routed Cut-off confirmed with the courier the same morning; the page now says "ordered by the 18th, collected or delivered by the 23rd".
02 Orders arrive by email to one inbox that two people read. Neither knows which ones the other has already baked. Routed Orders now print to a single sheet on the kitchen wall; whoever takes one initials it. The record lives where the work happens.
03 The website form could be edited by anyone with the shared login, including the seasonal staff member who does not set prices. Routed Price fields locked; the shared login can add stock but not change a price.
04 "We can make 40 boxes a day" came from a conversation in August and has been in the plan ever since. Nobody has made 40 in a day. Held Labelled as an estimate, not a capacity. Owner: the head baker, who will run one full day before the 18th and replace the figure with a measured one.
05 The whole page was waiting on a photographer who is away until the 20th. Routed Page released on Friday with two existing photographs. The new photographs drop in later without moving the launch.

Every entry above is fictional and is here to show the shape of a filled row: a specific finding, one of three dispositions, and either what was done or who now owns it.

06 If a box sells out, the form simply says "unavailable" and stops. A customer who has driven the decision that far is given nothing to do next. Routed Now reads: "Sold out for the 23rd. The 30th is open, and we will hold one for you if you reply to this message."

08 A gift-message card was designed last year, paid for, printed, and never offered to anybody. Routed Added to the page as a free option on every box. Cost already spent; value recovered on Friday.
09 If a card payment fails halfway, the order vanishes and the customer is shown a generic error. Two people did this last year and never came back. Open No safe route before Friday. The bakery will not lose the order: failed payments now email the order to the shop inbox marked HELD — PAYMENT NOT TAKEN, and a person replies the same day.

The verdict

ReleaseNot this. One item is open.
Route first Taken. The open item (09) had a safe manual route: hold the order, tell a person, reply the same day. Page went live Friday.
HoldNot required — no open item touched rights, safety or someone else's money without a route.

What the check was actually worth

Eleven minutes bought: a delivery promise the bakery can keep (01), a kitchen that cannot bake the same order twice (02), one fewer piece of personal data to look after (07), a printed card earning instead of sitting in a cupboard (08), and a failed payment that now reaches a human instead of disappearing (09).

It also produced the thing nobody has on the bad day: a written record of what was known on 14 November, and who owned the one figure still unproved.

Provenance

The nine inspections are the consumer form of the QYRIS gap discipline used inside THYLORA production, where a single workroom ran twenty-eight inspections against its own design and routed twenty-four of them before release. This packet reduces that discipline to nine inspections a person can run alone, in under ten minutes, on work that has nothing to do with software.

Nothing in the worked example is real. Halden Street Bakery does not exist, and no customer, price or supplier in it refers to anyone.
