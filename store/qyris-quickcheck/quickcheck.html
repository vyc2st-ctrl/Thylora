What this isPage 2

What it is, and what it is not.

It is

A pass over one decision. One decision, one sheet. Not your life, not your quarter, not your five-year plan.

A way of separating what you saw from what you were told, what you assumed and what you are hoping. Most decisions do not fail on logic. They fail because one link in the chain was hope wearing the clothes of a fact.

Auditable. The verdict comes from five ordered rules. The sheet prints the rule that fired. If you think the rule is wrong, you can say exactly which one and why.

Re-runnable. A hold is not a no. It names the missing fact. You go and get it, and you run the sheet again.

It is not

Not a score. Nothing here gets rated out of ten. A number invented from nothing is the exact thing this replaces.

Not a personality read. It asks nothing about you. It asks about the decision.

Not advice. It does not know your situation. It applies your own answers to a fixed table and shows its working.

Not a brake. Eight of the forty-eight possible input combinations return a clean Proceed, and ten more return Proceed with safeguard. It is built to let you move, with your eyes open.

The one rule of use

Fill it in before you commit, not after. Filled in afterwards it is a justification, and a justification always passes.

Where the name comes from

QYRIS is the working discipline behind it: Question, Yield, Reason, Inspect, Safeguard. The five moves are the discipline applied to one decision at a time. Nothing in the method depends on knowing that.

The twelve minutesPage 3

Twelve minutes, spent unevenly on purpose.

The two long moves are Reason and Inspect, because that is where decisions actually break. If you find yourself spending eight minutes on Question, you have not got a decision yet — you have got a worry.

Alone or with one other

Two people is the best size. A second person is the cheapest instrument you own for finding a weak link.

Written, not thought

Hand or keyboard, it does not matter. Unwritten answers drift while you are writing the next one.

Once per decision

If the decision changes shape — smaller, later, split in two — that is a new decision. New sheet.

Visual grammarPage 4

Four marks, used the same way every time.

Every diagram in this document is built from these four. Once you know them you can read any page of the sheet at a glance, including someone else’s filled-in copy.

Amber
Amber means one thing: the weakest part. It is never decoration and never a warning of danger. Where you see amber, that is the place the decision is most likely to break. It appears at most once per diagram.
Chain
A chain is a reasoning chain. Thick links are premises you can point at. The thin amber link is the one you would least want tested. A chain is exactly as strong as its thinnest link — never the average of its links.
Ladder
A ladder is the evidence ladder, and it always reads bottom-up: solid rung = observed, dashed = reported, dotted = assumed, faint = hoped. Height on the ladder is not quality of thinking. It is distance from something you could actually check.
Bracket
A bracket with a bar across it is a safeguard: the bracket is the decision, the bar is the thing that stops it. A bracket drawn without its bar is a decision with no stop rule.
Why the ladder is the whole trick

Almost nobody confuses observed with hoped when they are forced to write them in different places. Nearly everybody confuses them while thinking.

Move onePage 5
Q  Question · 2 minutes

State it as a choice between things you could actually do.

Most bad decisions are bad questions. “Should I do something about this?” has no answer. “Do I do A, or B, or wait thirty days?” has three.

Plain
What exactly are you choosing between? Write the options down.
Everyday
Write it as “Do I do A, or B?” with A and B being things you could start on Monday. If you can only name one option, you are not deciding, you are bracing yourself.
Technical
Express the decision as a set of mutually exclusive, individually executable alternatives, including the null alternative. An option set of size one is a commitment already made and should be checked as such.
The test for this move

Can you name at least two options, and could a stranger start on either one tomorrow without asking you a question? If not, keep writing.

Three things that fail this test

What gets writtenWhy it fails
“Whether to deal with the supplier situation.”No options. This is a topic.
“Whether to be smarter about spending.”Nobody can start it tomorrow.
“Whether to take the offer.”One option. The alternative is unwritten, so it will not get checked.

Always include wait as an option if waiting is genuinely available, and say how long. “Wait” with no end date is not an option, it is a mood.

Move twoPage 6
Y  Yield · 2 minutes

What it gives you if it works. What it takes if it does not.

In the same units, where units exist. Money against money, weeks against weeks. A yield in money against a cost in dignity cannot be compared, and pretending otherwise is how people talk themselves into things.

Plain
What do you get, and what does it cost you if you are wrong?
Everyday
Write both numbers. Then ask the only question that matters here: if the bad case happens in full, are you still standing?
Technical
Compare expected yield against maximum drawdown, not against expected loss. Probability-weighting an outcome you cannot survive is a category error: you do not get to play the average, you get the outcome.
The test for this move

Write the worst realistic case — not the worst imaginable one — in full. Then answer yes or no: can you absorb that? That yes or no goes straight into the readout.

Move threePage 7
R  Reason · 3 minutes

Write the chain. Then point at the thinnest link.

Plain
Why do you think this will work?
Everyday
Say it out loud as “because… and because… therefore…”. Then point at the because you would least want a stranger to test.
Technical
Decompose the decision into a premise chain terminating in the action. Chain strength is the minimum over premise strengths, not the mean. Identify the argmin premise; that premise, not the conclusion, is what the rest of the check is about.
The test for this move

Three becauses is usually enough. If you cannot get to three, the chain is short because it is unexamined, not because it is simple. If you need nine, you are defending rather than reasoning.

Carry the thinnest link forward. It is the only thing from this move that the readout uses. Everything else you wrote here was to find it.

Move fourPage 8
I  Inspect · 3 minutes

Put the thinnest link on the ladder. Honestly.

This is the move the whole sheet exists for. Take the because you pointed at, and find which rung it is standing on.

RungIt is here when…What moves it down a rung
ObservedYou saw it, or it is in writing in front of you right now.Nothing. It is already as low as it goes.
ReportedSomeone who would actually know told you.Ask for it in writing. Usually takes one message.
AssumedIt is reasonable, and nobody has checked.Name the cheapest check that would settle it, and its price.
HopedYou need it to be true, and you have nothing.Nothing cheap. This is why hoped always returns a hold.
Plain
How do you know that?
Everyday
Sort what you “know” into four piles: saw it, was told it, assumed it, hoping it. Your thinnest link sits in exactly one pile.
Technical
Classify the argmin premise by evidence provenance rather than by confidence. Self-reported confidence correlates poorly with provenance class, which is precisely why the class, not the confidence, is the input to the readout.
Then write the disconfirmer

One sentence: what would have to be true for me to drop this? If nothing you can name would change your mind, you are not checking a decision, you are announcing one.

Move fivePage 9
S  Safeguard · 2 minutes

What protects you if the thin link snaps.

A stop rule is one sentence with two halves: the observation that makes you stop, and what you do instead. “I’ll keep an eye on it” is not a stop rule. “If the inspection finds a second fault over $800, I do not repair — I sell as-is” is one.

Plain
If you are wrong, how do you get out, and when do you decide to?
Everyday
Write the stop rule down and tell one person who is not invested in the answer. A stop rule nobody else knows about gets quietly revised at the worst moment.
Technical
Pre-commit an exit condition on an observable, not on a feeling, and externalise it to a witness. The function of the witness is to raise the cost of silent revision at the point where your judgement is least reliable.
The test for this move

Read your stop rule to someone and ask them: “Would you know when this had happened?” If they hesitate, it is not observable yet.

The readoutPage 10

Five rules, in order. The first one that matches is your answer.

Four things come out of the five moves: the rung your thinnest link stands on, the reversal cost, whether you can absorb the bad case, and whether a stop rule is written. That is all the table needs.

RuleFires whenVerdictBecause
R1You cannot absorb the bad case and you cannot undo it.DeclineTwo failures that are survivable apart are not survivable together.
R2Your thinnest link is hoped.Hold for one more factHope is not an evidence class. One fact usually moves it, and one fact is cheap.
R3You cannot absorb the bad case, though you could undo it.Hold for one more factShrink it until you can absorb it. The smaller version is a different decision — run it again.
R4It is hard to undo, or your thinnest link is assumed.Proceed with safeguardEither is survivable with a stop rule. Neither is survivable without one.
R5Nothing above fired.ProceedThe chain holds on evidence you can point at, and being wrong is affordable.
A hold is not a no

Twenty-two of the forty-eight combinations return a hold, and a hold always names the missing fact. Most holds cost one phone call or one inspection and then the sheet is run again.

Worked example · onePage 11

A real, ordinary decision, filled in properly

The transmission quote is $4,200. The car is worth about $6,800.

Q · Question

A — pay $4,200 for the transmission repair and keep the car.
B — sell it as-is for about $1,900 and buy a replacement at about $9,500.
C — do nothing for thirty days and use the second car.
Three options. Each one could be started tomorrow. “Wait” has an end date.

Y · Yield

If it works: about three more years of use, no new car payment. Roughly $5,300 better than option B over those three years.
If it fails: $4,200 spent and still needing to replace — about $13,700 in total, against $9,500 if I go straight to B.
Can I absorb $4,200 going nowhere? Yes. It hurts. It does not end anything.
Both sides in dollars. The absorb answer is a plain yes.

R · Reason

Because the transmission is the only fault the shop has named,
and because the rest of the car has about three years left in it,
and because $4,200 for three years beats $9,500 for the same three years,
therefore I repair it.

Worked example · twoPage 12

I · Inspect — the thinnest link on the ladder

Observed: the written quote; the mileage on the clock.
Reported: the shop says the engine is sound.
Assumed: no second major failure in the next three years. ← this is the thin link
Hoped: nothing in this decision rests on hope, now that it is written out.
Rung: ASSUMED. And an assumption is checkable — that is the difference between R4 and R2.
What would change my mind: any second fault over $800 found before I authorise the work.
Cheapest check that settles it: a $180 pre-repair inspection of engine, suspension and rust.
$180 to move a $4,200 decision off an assumption. This is the moment the sheet earns its price.

S · Safeguard

Reversal cost: HIGH. Once the shop is paid, that money is gone.
Stop rule: if the $180 inspection finds a second fault over $800, I do not repair — I go to option B.
Told: the shop, written on the work order; and my partner.
Proceed
with safeguard
Rule R4 fired

Why: it is hard to undo and the thinnest link is an assumption. Either one alone would have triggered R4.

What that means here: the $180 inspection and the written stop rule go in before the work is authorised, not after.

What the sheet actually did: it turned “just fix it” into a $180 test that can change the answer. That is the whole return on twelve minutes.

Note what did not happen

The sheet did not tell anyone what to do with their car. It took four answers the owner already had and applied a fixed rule to them — and named the rule, so the owner can disagree with R4 rather than with a number.
