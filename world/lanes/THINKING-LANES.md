### 4.2 Case: Queen Charlotte and the Madragana descent

Held as a **primary subject** under the Chairman's standing relevance
directive, not as a side note, and entered in the lane in full — claim,
counter-claim, and the state of the evidence.

**Site relevance:** Queen Charlotte, consort of George III, was resident in the
Windsor orbit; the Windsor mirror is the reason this line surfaces here rather
than in an unrelated file.

**The claim.** Mario de Valdes y Cocom argued that Charlotte of
Mecklenburg-Strelitz was directly descended from **Margarita de Castro y
Sousa**, a fifteenth-century Portuguese noblewoman connected to the
thirteenth-century ruler **Afonso III** and **Madragana**, described in that
line as his Moorish partner. From this follows the assertion that the British
royal line — Charlotte, Victoria, and onward — carries African ancestry.
(Q1, Q2, Q3)

**The counter-claim, stated at equal strength.** Critics raise three distinct
objections, and they are not the same objection:
1. **Genealogical distance.** The descent is roughly **fifteen generations**.
   Even if every link held, the genetic contribution at that remove is
   negligible, and would not produce distinguishing physical features. (Q1)
2. **Identity of Madragana.** It is **not established** that Madragana was of
   recent African origin. Many scholars hold she was more probably a
   **Mozarab** — from the pre-Muslim population of Iberia, itself a mixture of
   Celtic, Roman and Germanic peoples. (Q1, Q4)
3. **Portraiture.** Readings of Charlotte's portraits are contested and are
   not independent evidence of descent.

**Where the historians actually sit.** The record shows them **divided**, not
unanimous in either direction. Some hold that she was Britain's first Black
queen and that her descendants therefore carry African ancestry; others hold
the generational distance makes the claim untenable. Reporting that presents
either side as settled is misreporting the state of the field. (Q3)

**Bias note — the lane's actual job.** Two opposite distortions are both live
on this question, and the lane records both:
- Popular-culture treatment (the *Bridgerton* effect) has pushed the claim
  toward **stated fact** in general circulation.
- Dismissals have at times been framed with a contempt — "ridiculous," "utterly
  mad" — that **exceeds what the genealogical objection itself supports**, and
  that tone is itself evidence about how the question is handled.

**Honest position of this lane:** the **genealogical line to Madragana is
documented**; **Madragana's own origin is disputed**; **the genetic inference
at fifteen generations is weak**; and the **claim is neither established nor
refuted**. It is **open**, and it is open on identifiable, statable grounds —
not on vibes.

**Verification gap — the single most important one in this file.** The primary
source of the claim, **de Valdes y Cocom's own PBS *Frontline* material (Q5)**,
was **not retrieved this run**. Every source held is a *summariser* of the
claim, including the sources that reject it. **No position on this case should
be hardened before the origin document is read at source.**

**Next action:** retrieve Q5 at primary. Then separate the case into three
independently resolvable questions — (a) the documented genealogy,
(b) Madragana's origin, (c) the genetic inference — and grade each on its own
evidence rather than as one package.
