## What the suite is

The royal kitchen does not stand alone. It is one room in a connected service
suite, and it cannot do its work without the rooms around it.

| Room | What it is for |
|---|---|
| **Royal Kitchen** | The cooking floor. Prep, hearth, range, spit. Holds no store beyond the working day. |
| **Scullery** | Dirty return, wash, rinse and dry. No cooking happens here, ever. |
| **Cold / wet store** | Fish, dairy, cut meat. Kept cold by mass and orientation, not by machinery. |
| **Larder** | The day larder — what today's cooking has already drawn. |
| **Bake / oven room** | Bread, pastry and oven work, with its own ash route. |
| **Herb store** | Dry herb below, a drying loft above. No water and no fire in the room, deliberately. |
| **Receiving** | Where everything entering the suite is weighed and checked before it is anywhere else. |

Two further spaces exist in the suite and are **not described on this sheet**.

## The four paths

**Food.** Delivery → receiving → weigh and check → store → issue → prep → cook →
service. Nothing reaches a store unweighed. That single rule is what makes the
kitchen's records mean anything.

**Herb.** Ground → cut → carried in → cleaned → either used fresh, or dried in the
loft, stripped to leaf and stored in lidded crocks → a day's measure to the kitchen
→ spent stalk to compost. The store crock stays in the store; only a day travels.

**Object.** Every tool has one home. Pots and pans live in the kitchen and wash in
the scullery. Baskets wash where the herb is cleaned. The head cook's own knife is
washed and dried by her, at the bench, and never sent to the wash troughs — a
wooden handle left to soak is how a handle is lost.

**Work.** The head cook sets the day list. The storekeeper issues against it. The
scullion holds the wash line. The baker holds the ovens and the ash. The herb
keeper holds cutting, drying and the crocks. When the head cook is off, the deputy
cook holds kitchen authority.

## What this sheet does not contain

Room sizes, wall thicknesses, heights, door and window counts, doorway clearances,
which room adjoins which, the routes between them, the service corridor, the stores
under key, the staff connection, and anything about access, locking or recall.

That is not an omission. Layout is how a building is entered.
