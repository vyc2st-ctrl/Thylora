### Option 1 — Grandparent raising the child *(my recommendation)*

- **Why together at homework time:** the grandparent is the household's only adult; homework is at the kitchen table because that is where the light is and where they are already sitting.
- **Routine:** meal, table cleared, books out, radio off. Same table, same hour, most days.
- **Dynamic:** the grandparent left formal schooling early and carries a quiet fear of being unable to help. That fear is *why they ask instead of explain* — which is exactly the canon behaviour `ASK_BEFORE_EXPLAINING` and `math_teaching_skill = NOT_ASSUMED`.
- **Why the story happens naturally:** they **cannot** supply the answer. The only move available to them is to ask the child what the sentence means. The story's whole discovery is forced by the character's limitation rather than by authorial convenience. **This is the option where the backend's existing fields do the most work.**

### Option 2 — Working parent, single-adult household

- **Why together:** homework lands in the gap between the end of a shift and supper.
- **Routine:** compressed, clock-watching, interrupted by cooking.
- **Dynamic:** the parent's instinct is to correct fast and move on. Time pressure is the antagonist.
- **Why the story happens naturally:** the parent nearly marks it wrong themselves — then stops. The discovery comes from an adult catching their own reflex. Strong, but it needs the adult to be *unusually* reflective, which fights `PATIENT_BUT_REAL`.

### Option 3 — Non-parent relative with care responsibility

- **Why together:** the child lives with them; arrangement's history is not on the page.
- **Routine:** slightly formal, negotiated rather than assumed.
- **Dynamic:** affectionate but without the accumulated friction of a parent-child school history.
- **Why the story happens naturally:** the child will admit "I don't know what that means" *sooner*, because there is less shame in the room. Gets to the discovery fastest — but the fast admission weakens the demonstration, because the story's force comes from how long the misreading went unnoticed.

**Chairman decision required.** Option 1 makes the adult's limitation the engine; Option 2 makes time
the engine; Option 3 makes safety the engine. They are not cosmetic variants.

## H. The math problem *(authored here, original)*

> ### P_solve = L × M × S
>
> | Term | Meaning (LOCKED canon) |
> |---|---|
> | **L** | Do I understand the sentence? |
> | **M** | Do I understand the mathematical relationship? |
> | **S** | Can I solve it? |
>
> *If L = 0, do not infer math inability; repair the language layer first.*

### The problem, exact

> **The mill sends a load of flour to the bakehouse every other day. Each load is 8 sacks. The
> bakehouse uses 5 sacks a day. Over 6 days, does the flour in the store room go up or down?**

### The blocker

**Exact blocking phrase: "every other day."**

| | |
|---|---|
| **What the learner takes it to mean** | *Every day* — or *every day other than the first*. Both readings are available in ordinary speech and neither is flagged as unusual. |
| **What it means here** | Every **second** day. Over 6 days that is **3** loads, not 6. |
| **Why it is invisible** | It is not a mathematical term. It will not appear in a glossary, it is not bolded, and the learner has heard it spoken many times without ever needing it to be precise. |

### Why this phrase and not another

**The misreading flips the answer.** That is the test of a real blocker.

- Correct reading: 3 loads × 8 = **24 in**; 6 days × 5 = **30 out** → **down by 6**.
- Misreading: 6 loads × 8 = **48 in**; **30 out** → **up by 18**.

Same arithmetic. Opposite conclusions. A phrase with no numbers in it decided the answer.

### Pure mathematical structure, language removed

> **Add 8 three times. Subtract 5 six times. Is the result above or below zero?**
>
> `24 − 30 = −6` → below zero → **down**.

### The diagnostic move

Give the learner the stripped version. If they complete it — **M = 1, S = 1, L = 0**, and the
original wrong answer was never evidence about mathematics.

**Do not diagnose math ability from an L = 0 event.** And symmetrically: completing the stripped
version does not prove the learner would have managed the worded one. It establishes M and S, and
leaves L as the open question — which is the point.
