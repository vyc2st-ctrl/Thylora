# Tea Set Talk — the nightly family game

**Built around Grandma's tea set.** Same cups, every night, so the girls grow up
knowing the set means "we sit down and we hear each other". One day they pour
from it for their own grandkids.

## The pour (5 minutes to set up, 20–30 minutes to play)

1. **Nobody pours their own cup.** Each person pours for the person on their
   left. That's the first act of the night: you served someone.
2. **The teapot is the talking piece.** Whoever has the pot in front of them
   talks. Everyone else listens — no phones at the table.
3. **Three rounds, named for the tea:**

| Round | Question | What it gets you |
|---|---|---|
| **Sweet** | What was the best part of your day? | Starts every night on something good |
| **Bitter** | What was hard, annoying, or not fair today? | The real stuff, said out loud, safely |
| **Steep** | What's still brewing — something you're waiting on, worried about, or excited for tomorrow? | What to check on tomorrow night |

4. **The Grandmother's Cup** (last round, one person per night, rotating):
   draw a card and ask it. Answers go to the **Root House** as oral history —
   whoever asked gets credited on the Credit Wall as `REMEMBERED` / `FOUND`.
   Jordan, Cali and Key become the family's junior researchers.

## Grandmother's Cup cards (start deck)

- What was your grandmother's full name, and what did people call her?
- Where was she born? Did anyone ever say "Canada"?
- What church did the family go to, and in what town?
- What did your grandfather do for work?
- What food did your grandmother make that nobody makes the same now?
- Who in the family moved away, and where did they go?
- What's a word or saying only our family uses?
- Who's buried where? Which cemetery?
- Is there a family Bible, funeral program, or old photo box? Who has it?
- What's a story you were told that you were never sure was true?

## Bonus rules

- **Spill the Tea (Fridays):** one honest question to anyone at the table; they
  can answer or pass with "still steeping."
- **Refill:** if someone helped you today, you refill their cup and say how.
- **Creature Feature after tea:** the girls want the classics — *Frankenstein*
  (1931), *Bride of Frankenstein* (1935), *Dracula* (1931) — and *The Conjuring 2*.
  Popcorn, fruit, and one Grandmother's Cup question during the credits:
  *"What scared you when you were our age?"*
