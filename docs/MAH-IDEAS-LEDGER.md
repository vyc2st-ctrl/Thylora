### The gap you saw between the logic types, made into a tool

You're right that they **are** interchangeable in practice. Each one leaves a gap the next one fills:

| Type | What it asks | The gap you spotted | What bridges it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deduction | "If these rules are true, what must follow?" | Knowing "every artifact needs a serial" isn't the same as *checking* the serial | A **precheck** that runs the rule (`mah/lib/precheck.js`) |
| Induction | "What patterns repeat?" | What is the pattern made of, and who else confirmed it? | A **second independent observer** before trusting it |
| Abduction | "What explanation best fits the evidence?" | Where did the evidence come from? One source? | **Two or more sources** looking for the explanation |
| Counterfactual | "What happens if this condition changes?" | You asked "what actually produced the result?" | Measure the **mechanism**, not just before/after |
| Bayesian | "How should new evidence change my confidence?" | Failure was found at the ninth hour | Run checks **before** the work, so confidence starts earned |
| Constraint | "What must be true before something can proceed?" | You said you rarely practice this | That's exactly what a precheck list is. It practices constraint logic for you |

**Bayesian isn't a person's method by name only:** it comes from **Rev. Thomas Bayes** (c. 1701–1761), an English minister and mathematician. His essay was published after his death (1763) by Richard Price.

**On reading in your logic vs. theirs:** you write in your logic, they read in theirs. The bridge is **the question**. A statement gets translated into the reader's logic, but a well-aimed question makes them *run your logic themselves* to answer it. That's why every report now leads with an invoked question and gives the math second.

## 7 · Penny Candy (story draft, VYC Original)

> Every penny I got, I saved. Not to keep, but to count.
> At the store on the corner, near 33 and 77 in Batavia, I'd put my pennies on the counter and say, "Twelve pieces of gum." The girl behind the counter counted them out. Twelve.

> She'd been smiling at the TV. She turned, saw it was me, and took a breath. Then she saw the pile, and she knew I was serious.
> "Just give me the whole bag," I said. They wouldn't. "Then give me the whole box." Mike, the owner, wouldn't sell me the box either. I had the money. He still said no.
> I learned something that day that I didn't have words for until later: **people get uneasy when a child takes them at their word.**

> "Did you wash your hands?" I asked.

## 17 · Food: cereal and the spicy chip

**Wheat cereal:** whole wheat + nuts, toasted in the oven, no additives. Optional cinnamon or other natural herbs.
**Spicy chip (refined from your recipe):** slice potatoes very thin (1/16"). Soak **30 minutes to overnight in the fridge**, not days (multi-day soaks go mushy and can spoil), in lemon juice, salt, pepper, a pinch of sugar, red pepper flakes and garlic salt. Pat dry. Bake at 400 °F (200 °C) for 15–25 minutes, or dehydrate and finish in the oven. Toss with **just a hint of honey in the last 2 minutes** (honey burns early). Finish with sea salt. Four or five chips per small glass jar on the counter, next to two or three fruit snacks.

## 24 · When you need a study, and when you don't (the pesticide thought)
- **Observation finds the signal.** You hear people repeat the same answer; you don't need a study to know growth hasn't happened.
