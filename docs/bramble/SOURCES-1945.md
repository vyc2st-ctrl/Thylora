## 1 · Labelling law

Every factual line in every Bramble EP001 document carries one of three marks.

| Mark | Meaning | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `[E]` | **Earth documented.** A real fact about 1945 England. | Must cite a source ID below. May not be altered to suit the story. |
| `[S]` | **EdereAriah simulation design.** Invented for this production. | Must never be presented, on screen or in supporting material, as Earth history. |
| `[E→S]` | **Simulation modelled on a documented Earth practice.** A fictional instance of a real, sourced pattern. | Must cite the source ID of the pattern it is modelled on, and be named as fictional. |

The town, the school, the teacher, every child, every family, every shop and
every serial number in this bible are `[S]` or `[E→S]`. **No real school, real
child, real family or real business is depicted.** Where a real company is named
in this register it is named only as the *documented pattern* a fictional
supplier is modelled on — never as a supplier to the fictional school.

## 3 · Prime Directive section — Black presence in 1945 England

This section is carried at **primary-subject depth**, not as a footnote, because it
bears directly on who is in the classroom, who is in the town, and what the
record does and does not say. It is separated into evidence, counter-evidence,
and reporting bias, and every load-bearing claim is sourced.

### 3.1 · The documented evidence

| ID | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|

### 3.2 · The honest counter-evidence

This production does not overstate its own case. Three qualifications are
recorded and must be held:

1. **Distribution, not absence.** The documented Black population of 1945
   England was concentrated in **port cities** — Liverpool, Cardiff, London,
   Bristol, South Shields (`S-28`, `S-29`). A **small inland market town** would
   in ordinary times have had a very small Black population or none. Placing a
   Black British child in such a town is therefore not justified by simply
   asserting one; it needs a documented route.
2. **The route is documented.** The route used by this production is
   **evacuation and billeting**, which moved city children — including children
   of long-settled port communities — into rural reception areas and small towns
   in Cheshire, North Wales, Lancashire and **Shropshire** (`S-26`), and which
   left an estimated 5,200 unaccompanied children still evacuated as late as
   March 1946 (`S-25`). A Merseyside-born Black child, evacuated to a Shropshire
   town after the 1941 bombing of Merseyside and not returned by late 1945, sits
   **inside** the documented pattern rather than beside it.
3. **Age arithmetic rules out one route.** The "brown babies" (`S-31`) were born
   from roughly **1943 onward**, so in November 1945 they are **infants and
   toddlers — not schoolchildren**. They may legitimately appear in the town as
   babies, in a pram, in a billet, in an adoption thread. They **may not** appear
   as classmates in EP001. Getting this wrong would be exactly the kind of
   unsourced invention this register exists to prevent.

### 3.3 · Known bias in the mainstream depiction

The following is recorded as a **production standard**, and as a statement about
how the period is usually reported rather than about what the period was:

- Popular period drama and most school-facing "1945 classroom" material depict
  the English classroom as uniformly white. The sources above show that the
  underlying population of 1945 England was **not** uniformly white: a Black
  community in Liverpool with roots to the 1730s and ten traceable generations
  (`S-28`), a settled multi-origin community in Butetown from the 1800s with its
  own institutions by the 1940s (`S-29`), around 5,400 West Indian RAF personnel
  arriving in the last year of the war alone (`S-30`), and roughly 240,000
  African-American servicemen in the country (`S-31`).
- The erasure is therefore a **depiction habit, not a demographic fact.** The
  correct correction is not to insert a Black child arbitrarily into every
  English scene — that would substitute one unsourced picture for another — but
  to route each character through a **documented** presence, name the route, and
  let the record carry the weight.
- The same record shows the discrimination plainly (`S-32`). A production that
  shows Black presence without showing the colour bar is as incomplete as one
  that shows neither. EP001 is a children's mystery about record-keeping and is
  **not** built as a story about racism; the colour bar is carried as
  **background truth available to later episodes**, not as EP001's subject.

**Unresolved and held for Chairman decision:** whether, and in which episode, the
colour bar (`S-32`) and the "brown babies" thread (`S-31`) are brought forward as
foreground material. Neither is used as foreground in EP001.
