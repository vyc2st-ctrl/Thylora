# BRAMBLE EP001 · The World — Corvesley, Shropshire, 1945

**All place, person and business names in this file are `[S]` simulation design.**
Corvesley is **not** a real settlement and is not identified with one. Earth facts
about 1945 England carry `[E]` and a source ID from `SOURCES-1945.md`.

---

## 1 · The town

### 1.1 · Character brief

Corvesley is an **attractive but ordinary** small English market town — a stone
bridge, a market place, a church, a mill, a branch-line station, four hundred-odd
households, and a great deal of mud in November.

**Binding tone rules `[S]`:**

- **Not fantasy.** Nothing in Corvesley is impossible, magical or heightened.
- **Not exaggerated poverty.** People are rationed, mended, thrifty and tired.
  They are **not** ragged, starving or picturesque. Houses are clean. Children
  are fed. Boots are repaired rather than absent.
- **Not Dickens caricature.** No grotesques, no beadles, no comic cruelty, no
  Victorian pastiche. The adults are competent and the institutions work.
- **Wartime residue, not wartime peril.** The war in Europe ended in May `[E] S-22`
  and the war with Japan in August `[E] S-24`. Corvesley in November 1945 is
  **tired, relieved and short of everything** — which is the texture, not the plot.

### 1.2 · Placement `[S]` on a documented pattern `[E→S]`

Corvesley is placed in **Shropshire**, in the belt that received evacuated
children from Merseyside. That belt is documented: Liverpool's evacuation
reception areas included Cheshire, North Wales, Lancashire, Shrewsbury and
Shropshire, and about 3,300 children and 900 mothers went to Oswestry alone on
special trains from Liverpool `[E] S-26`. Evacuees returned through 1945, but the
scheme did not formally end until 31 March 1946, with an estimated 5,200
unaccompanied children still evacuated `[E] S-25`.

That is the documented route by which a Merseyside family reaches Corvesley and
is still there in November 1945. **The route is named because the character
depends on it** — see §3.4 and `SOURCES-1945.md` §3.2.

This matters: the town's losses are absences — men not yet home, a name on the
memorial — not rubble. Rubble would pull the design toward spectacle.

**Why Bramble asks why `[S]`:** his household runs on paperwork that does not
match the world. Ration books, coupons, a service pay-book, letters that arrive
out of order, a father who is officially somewhere and actually nowhere. Nell's
biscuit tin is the first archive Bramble ever saw. **He learned that a written
figure and a real thing are two different objects before he learned arithmetic.**

**Naming — UNRESOLVED (`U-04`):** whether "Bramble" is the boy's surname (as
recorded here, which makes the class register read `BRAMBLE, Thomas E.`) or a
nickname over a different surname. Held for the Chairman.

### 3.4 · Emanuel — DOCUMENTARY EVIDENCE

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Address | **11 Wenlow Row**, Corvesley (billet since 1941; the family stayed on) |
| Child | **Pearl Emanuel**. Born Liverpool, 1936. Age 9. |
| Mother | **Ivy Emanuel**, 31. Liverpool-born. Works at the post office counter. |
| Father | **Joseph Emanuel**, 38. **Ship's donkeyman, Merchant Navy**, sailing out of Liverpool. Home between voyages; at sea in November 1945. |
| Grandmother | **Hannah Emanuel**, 63, in Liverpool. Liverpool-born; her own family Liverpool-settled for generations. |
| Billet host | **Mrs Gwen Tasker**, 11 Wenlow Row, widow. Received the billeting allowance from 1941 `[E→S] S-26`. |

**Documented route — stated, not assumed `[E]`:**

- Liverpool has **Britain's oldest and longest-established Black community,
  dating to the 1730s**, with some families tracing Black heritage through as
  many as **ten generations**; early arrivals included sailors `[E] S-28`.
- Liverpool's children were evacuated to reception areas **including Shropshire**
  `[E] S-26`; the scheme did not end until **31 March 1946**, with thousands of
  children still evacuated `[E] S-25`.
- Pearl is therefore in Corvesley by the **documented evacuation route**, from a
  **documented community**, in a **documented year**. She is not inserted; she is
  routed. See `SOURCES-1945.md` §3.2 for the counter-evidence held alongside this.

**Why Pearl reads records `[S]`:** she is a child whose existence in Corvesley is
**held together by paperwork** — a billeting form, an allowance, an admission
entry in a school she did not start at, a ration book registered to one address
and a home address in another city. Her mother works the post-office counter,
where the whole town's forms cross the desk. Pearl learned early that **the
record is what decides whether you are somewhere.**

**Held, not foregrounded:** colour bars operated in wartime England and were
tested in court in 1944 `[E] S-32`. EP001 does **not** use this as foreground.
It is background truth, available to later episodes (`U-05`).

## 6 · Clothing and the coupon economy `[E]` + `[S]`

**Documented `[E]`:** the clothing coupon allowance was **at its lowest in
1945–46** — 24 coupons for the eight months from 1 September 1945 to 30 April
1946, about three a month. Children's garments carried **lower coupon values**,
and from 1942 every child received an extra ten coupons. Rationing ran to 1949;
the CC41 Utility scheme to 1952 `[E] S-17`.

**Design consequences `[S]`, binding on costume:**

1. **Nothing in EP001 is new** except where a coupon has visibly been spent on it.
2. **Knitted items cost no coupons if the wool is re-used.** Ada Bramble unravels
   old jerseys and knits them again. This is the licence for Bramble's
   **deep-red winter cap with the soft pom** (C-1): the red wool is older than
   the cap, and the cap is therefore **period-honest and rationing-honest.**
3. **The deep-red scarf accents** (C-2) are the same wool, worked as bands or
   end-blocks — which is exactly why they are **selective** and not a whole red
   scarf. The household had that much red wool and no more.
4. Coats are handed down and let out. Sleeves are lengthened with a different
   cloth. Boots are mended at Bache's. Socks are darned in a visibly different
   shade.
5. **No child in EP001 is ragged.** Mended is not ragged. This is the line that
   separates the design from Dickens caricature (§1.1).

## 7 · Sanitation and refuse `[E]` + `[E→S]`

**Documented `[E]`:**

- The **Public Health Act 1936** introduced the **"standard receptacle"** for
  household waste — the galvanised metal dustbin, approved or supplied by the
  council and emptied by refuse workers on collection days `[E] S-18`.
- **Paper Salvage** ran from 1939; emergency legislation of **26 June 1940**
  required most councils to run effective salvage collections; local-authority
  salvage peaked at **433,405 tons in 1942, 49.6% of all collections** `[E] S-19`.
- Many councils replaced private bins with **communal bins moved onto the street,
  repainted and labelled for specific materials** `[E] S-19`.
- **From 1942 it was illegal to throw away or burn paper** `[E] S-12`.

**Corvesley arrangement `[E→S]`:**

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Authority | Corvesley Urban District Council, yard on Pinfold Lane |
| Foreman | **Walter Noakes** (Alfie's father) |
| Household refuse | Galvanised bins, weekly round `[E→S] S-18` |
| Salvage | **Four communal bins on Pinfold Lane**, repainted and stencilled: **PAPER · METAL · BONES · RAGS** `[E→S] S-19` |
| **School salvage** | The school keeps a **paper salvage sack** in the coke store. **The council salvage cart calls at the Pinfold Lane gate on Thursday mornings.** |
| Waste paper rule | **Nothing paper is burned.** Spoiled work, torn covers and old exercise books go to the salvage sack — because burning it is illegal `[E] S-12` |

> **This is the single most load-bearing environmental fact in EP001.** It means
> that a missing exercise book has an **entirely innocent and entirely legal**
> route out of the classroom — into a sack, onto a cart, out of the town — on
> **the very morning the episode is set** (Thursday 22 November 1945 `[E] S-27`).
> It is listed as one of eleven candidate explanations in
> `OBJECTS-AND-CUSTODY.md` §7 and **is not confirmed by this bible.**

## 8 · Transportation `[E]` + `[S]`

**Documented `[E]`:** the **basic petrol ration for civilians was restored on
1 June 1945** — around 150 miles a month — after private petrol had been
withdrawn completely in 1942 `[E] S-21`.

| Mode | Corvesley `[S]` |
|---|---|
| **Foot** | The dominant mode. Every child in EP001 walks to school. |
| **Bicycle** | The dominant adult mode. Pym's Cycle Works is a busier shop than the garage. Tyres are scarce; patches are normal. |
| **Bus** | A country service to the market town, **market days and Saturdays only**. Stop at the Market Place. |
| **Railway** | A single-track branch line and a small station on Station Road; goods yard, coal drops, milk traffic from the creamery. **Operating company not yet fixed — see `U-06`.** |
| **Horse** | Still working: the milk float, the coal cart, the brewer's dray, and **the council refuse and salvage cart** (§7). |
| **Motor** | Few and rationed `[E] S-21`: the doctor, the vet, the coal merchant's lorry, the creamery lorry, one taxi at the station. |
| **Carrier** | A carrier's cart to outlying farms twice a week. |

**Rule `[S]`:** the town is quiet. Sound design should be footsteps, hooves,
bicycle bells, a distant locomotive and the school bell — **not** traffic.
