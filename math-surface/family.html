Three different problems look identical on a marked page.

A wrong answer on a word problem has at least three possible causes, and they need completely different help. This page shows you which one it was.

The three layers, in plain words

L · Could they read it?Not "can they read" in general — can they read this sentence, with words like “at least”, “respectively” and “per” in it?
M · Did they know what was happening?Given the numbers, do they know whether this is a taking-away, a sharing, a comparing?
S · Could they do the working?The arithmetic itself, with no story attached.

All three have to hold. That is why they are multiplied rather than added:

L×M×S= P_solve

If any one of them is zero, the answer comes out wrong — and the page looks exactly the same in all three cases.

The sentence you are entitled to question

“They lack the mathematics.”

Ask one question back: how was that measured with the reading taken out? If a child could not read the sentence, the wrong answer tells you nothing at all about their mathematics — a child with perfect arithmetic produces exactly the same wrong answer. Until somebody hands them the numbers already pulled out of the sentence and asks what is happening to them, nobody has measured the mathematics.

The clearest example in the set

Ruth and Odell picked 14 and 9 pears respectively. Odell then gave 3 of his pears to Ruth. How many pears does Odell have now?

A child who misses one word — “respectively” — works out 14 − 3 = 11. The subtraction is perfect. The answer is about the wrong person.

Marked on the page, that is a wrong answer in mathematics. Measured properly, it is a reading result with the mathematics intact. Those two conclusions send a child down completely different roads for the next year.

What to ask at the school

  1. Was the language checked on its own — with no numbers in the question at all?
  2. Was the relationship checked with the numbers already pulled out, so the reading could not get in the way?
  3. Was the arithmetic checked with no story attached?
  4. Which of those three is the one holding the rest down?
  5. What was not measured — and what is therefore not being claimed?

These are not challenges. They are the five questions the model itself has to answer before it will say anything about a child, and a school that already works this way will have the answers to hand.

The ten words worth knowing at home

These are the words that decide whether a sentence can be read. You can check any of them at a kitchen table, with no numbers involved at all.

Try one together

Read the sentence. Before anybody works anything out, ask the reading question underneath it. That single habit is most of what this whole system does.

What an Understanding Card is, and is not

It is a record of what an adult actually watched, on one morning, in three separately measured layers — plus your child's own words about what the answer meant, kept exactly as they said them.

It is not a grade, a level, a diagnosis, or a prediction. Every card prints what was not measured, so that nothing can be quietly read into a gap.
