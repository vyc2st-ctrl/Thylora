THE MATHEMATICS OF UNDERSTANDING A PROBLEM

P_solve = L × M × S

Three separate things have to be true before a word problem can be solved. Multiplying them is not a metaphor — it is what makes the model useful.

L · LanguageDo I understand the sentence?
M · Mathematical relationshipDo I understand what is happening to the numbers?
S · SolveCan I do the working?

Why multiply and not add

If the three were added, being strong at two of them would carry you. They are multiplied because any one of them at zero takes the whole thing to zero. You cannot answer a sentence you cannot read, however good your arithmetic is.

L = 1, M = 1, S = 0 → P_solve = 0.   L = 0, M = 1, S = 1 → P_solve = 0.   The two zeros look identical on a marked page, and they need opposite help.

The rule that matters most

If L = 0, we do not conclude that the learner lacks the mathematics.

Zero times an unknown number is zero. That is true whatever the unknown number is — which is exactly why the zero tells you nothing about it. A learner who could not read the sentence produces the same zero whether their mathematics is perfect or absent. So when the sentence blocks, the mathematics has to be measured again with the words taken off, before anybody says a word about it.

That sentence was doing the blocking, not you. We have not found out anything about your mathematics yet, so we are going to look at that next, with the words taken off.

Measured, or not measured — never assumed

A layer only counts as measured when it was looked at on its own. A score from the mixed word problem is not evidence about any single layer, because all three were in the way at once.

We have not checked that part yet, so we are not going to say anything about it.
