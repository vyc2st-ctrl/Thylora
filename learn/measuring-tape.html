The tape: 0 to 12 inches

Tall lines are whole inches. Each shorter line is half the size of the step before it. Tap or click anywhere on the tape to read that spot. The coloured pins show the three worked examples.

On a phone, slide the tape sideways to see all 12 inches. Keyboard: focus the tape and use ← → (one sixteenth) or Shift + ← → (one inch).

Kid versionFold a strip of paper in half. Fold it in half again. And again. And again. Each fold cuts every piece into two equal pieces, so each piece is half as long as before. A tape measure does the same thing four times inside every inch: 1 → halves → quarters → eighths → sixteenths. The more folds a mark needed, the shorter its line.
Formal versionThe inch is divided by repeated bisection: the marks are at positions k/2n in for n = 0…4. A position s (in sixteenths) belongs to the class of the largest step that divides it: 16 | s → whole, 8 | s → half, 4 | s → quarter, 2 | s → eighth, otherwise sixteenth. Step sizes 16, 8, 4, 2, 1 sixteenths form a geometric sequence with ratio 1/2, which is why every fraction on the tape reduces to a denominator of 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 — never 3, 5 or 10.

Counting trickTo read a spot, count the little lines past the last big number. 8 inches and 12 little lines = 8 12/16. Then make it simpler: 12/16 = 6/8 = 3/4. So it is 8 3/4.

Math Display Law block: clearance

C = Wg − Wv

ENGINEERING_CONSTRAINT PRODUCTION_PLANNED

Symbols
C clearance (total, both sides) · Wg garage-opening width (subscript g = garage) · Wv vehicle width (subscript v = vehicle) · m minimum clearance per side · operator − subtraction; per side is C / 2
Units
All lengths in inches (in), held as whole sixteenths, and in millimetres (mm); 1 in = 25.4 mm exactly. The fit result is unitless (true/false).
Domain
Wg ≥ 0, Wv ≥ 0, m ≥ 0, each a tape reading to 1/16 in (1.5875 mm). C may be negative (car wider than opening).
Scale
Household doors and vehicles: roughly 60–216 in (1.5–5.5 m). Precision 1/16 in; per side is exact to 1/32 in.
Threshold
Fit when C / 2 ≥ m, tested as C ≥ 2m in whole sixteenths (no rounding). The calculator starts with m = 12 in (304.8 mm) per side as a teaching value — typical planning comfort, not a code requirement; verify locally.
Assumptions
Vehicle is centred and driven straight; Wg is the clear opening (inside the jambs and door track, not the rough framing); Wv includes whatever sticks out (mirrors unless folded); both are measured at the same height.
Failure condition
C < 0 → vehicle wider than opening (cannot pass). 0 ≤ C < 2m → passes only with less than the stated room per side (NO_FIT). Also fails if a reading is invalid (denominator not 1/2/4/8/16), if the car is not centred, or if a narrower obstacle (track, opener rail, shelving) sits inside the opening.
Plain meaning
Subtract the car's width from the door's width to get the spare room; split it in two for each side and compare it with the room you need.
Child meaning
How much space is left over when the car goes through the door? Take the door's size, take away the car's size, and share what's left between the two sides.
Worked example
Wg = 108 in (2743.2 mm, a 9 ft door) · Wv = 74 in (1879.6 mm) → C = 34 in (863.6 mm) → C / 2 = 17 in (431.8 mm) per side ≥ m = 12 in (304.8 mm) → FIT. Values are typical, verify locally.
Class

Where measuring shows up

Every size below is a typical Earth value for practice — typical, verify locally (codes, rules and products vary).

Garage design

A typical single garage door is 9 ft wide (108 in, 2.74 m) and 7 ft tall (84 in, 2.13 m); a typical double is 16 ft (192 in, 4.88 m). With a 74 in (1.88 m) car, a 9 ft door leaves 17 in (431.8 mm) each side.

Room design

A queen mattress is typically 60 × 80 in (1524 × 2032 mm). In a 10 ft (120 in, 3.05 m) wide room, that leaves 60 in (1524 mm) — 30 in (762 mm) of walkway each side if centred. Same subtraction as the garage.

Animal enclosure

Poultry runs are commonly wrapped in 1/2 in (12.7 mm) hardware cloth: small enough to keep many predators out. On the tape, 1/2 in is the half mark — 8/16. A typical 4 ft (48 in, 1.22 m) roll width sets the run's panel height.

Sports court

A basketball rim is set 10 ft (120 in, 3.048 m) above the floor. A full NBA court is 94 × 50 ft (28.65 × 15.24 m); school and park courts are often smaller. Measuring a 10 ft rim needs a tape to at least 120 in.

Vehicle fit

A typical mid-size car is about 72–74 in (1.83–1.88 m) wide without mirrors; mirrors often add several inches in total. Decide whether Wv includes mirrors before you subtract.

Store shelving

“3/4 in” plywood usually measures 23/32 in (18.3 mm) — a thirty-second, finer than this tape. A “2 × 4” board is actually 1 1/2 × 3 1/2 in (38.1 × 88.9 mm). Five 3 1/2 in faces side by side need 17 1/2 in (444.5 mm) of shelf.
