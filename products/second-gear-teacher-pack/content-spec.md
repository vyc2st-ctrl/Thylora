### Page 2 · How to use this pack
- **The two gears.** First gear: *what happens?* Second gear: *how do we know, and what would happen if…?*
- **Unit shape:** Lesson 1 (Cards 1–2) · Lesson 2 (Cards 3–4) · Lesson 3 (Cards 5–6) · Lesson 4 (Cards 7–8) · Lesson 5 (Cards 9–10 and assessment)
- **Time:** 30–45 minutes per lesson
- **Grouping:** detective squads of 3–4, with rotating roles: Lead Detective (reads the clue), Evidence Keeper (records), Skeptic (asks "how do we know?"), Reporter (shares out)

photosynthesis · chlorophyll · glucose · starch · carbon dioxide · oxygen · stomata · xylem · respiration · energy · evidence · prediction.

### Page 5 · Lesson 1–2 plan (Cards 1–4)
- **Hook:** "A tree can weigh as much as a truck, but it never eats."
- **Launch the investigation:** start the shoebox seedling (Card 2) and the celery (Card 3) on day 1, so results are ready by lesson 2.
- **Card routine (per card, about 8 minutes):** read the clue → squads predict → reveal the evidence → try it or discuss → second-gear question → Skeptic challenge
- **Exit ticket:** "One thing a plant needs, and how we know."

### Page 6 · Lesson 3–4 plan (Cards 5–8)
- **Recipe build (Card 5):** students assemble the equation from cut-apart ingredient tiles
- **Bubble count (Card 6):** light versus shade, with class data pooled on the board
- **Starch test (Card 7):** **teacher demo only** (iodine)
- **Chromatography (Card 8):** **teacher demo or adult-led small group** (alcohol)
- **Exit ticket:** "Where does the oxygen come from?" (Answer: water.)

### Page 7 · Lesson 5 plan (Cards 9–10) and assessment
- Day/night chart (Card 9)
- Breakfast-to-sun chain (Card 10), done individually
- Then the assessment on Page 9

### Page 8 · Investigation sheet (student-facing)
A structured page: **Question · My prediction · What I did · What I observed (drawing and words) · What it means · Second-gear question I still have**. It is designed for the shoebox seedling, celery, or bubble count.

### Page 9 · Assessment (student-facing)
- 5 first-gear items (multiple choice or matching: ingredients, products, where it happens, the green pigment, day/night)
- 3 second-gear items (short written answers):
  1. "A student says plants get their food from the soil. Use evidence to agree or disagree."
  2. "Predict what happens to the bubble count if we add a second lamp. Explain."
  3. "Trace a food you like back to the sun."
- A 3-level rubric for the second-gear items: **Clue-level** (a restated fact) · **Detective-level** (a fact plus a reason) · **Second-gear** (a fact, a reason, and a prediction or counter-example)

### Page 10 · Answer key and rubric exemplars
Answers for Page 9, plus one exemplar answer at each rubric level for each second-gear item.

### Page 11 · Safety, materials and differentiation
- **Materials list:** bean seeds, shoebox, celery, food colouring, clear bags, pondweed (for example elodea from an aquarium shop), a clear jar, a lamp, raw potato, apple, iodine solution (teacher only), rubbing alcohol (teacher only), coffee filters
- **Safety summary:** iodine is poisonous if swallowed and stains; rubbing alcohol is flammable (no flames, ventilate); plastic bags are a hazard for young children; supervise near water; check the class allergy list before handling plants
- **Differentiation:** *Support:* picture-supported vocabulary, sentence frames ("I think ___ because ___"). *Extension:* design a fair test that changes one variable; research chemosynthesis
- **Accessibility:** every investigation has an observe-only role, and colour-based results are also described in words

### Page 12 · Family letter and extension
- A half-page letter home: what we're learning, one at-home try-it (a breakfast-to-sun chain at the table), and no materials required
- An extension menu: autumn leaf pigments, a school garden plot, a "tree mass" estimate
