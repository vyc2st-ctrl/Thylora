# LONGER SHELF — the next five products

Ranked by readiness. All **PROPOSED**; none is approved, built, priced or scheduled.

## 1. HERB FILE 002 — KOLA (*Cola nitida* / *C. acuminata*)
Ranked second in the File 001 selection (S = 1280) and held deliberately for the second slot.
West African origin, documented ceremonial use, the trade that carried it, and the cola that
made it globally famous while making its origin invisible. Strong on the shelf's core work:
separating what is documented about a plant's origin from how the mainstream account tells
it. Needs a West African provenance research lane opened — which is why it is 002, not 001.

## 2. HERB FILE 003 — CINCHONA (quinine)
The bark that changed who could survive where, and therefore changed history. Andean origin,
the extraction of the plant and the knowledge, the Java monopoly, wartime scramble. The
shelf's first genuinely hard file: the record on pre-Columbian indigenous use is contested,
and the file's job is to report the contest accurately rather than pick the pleasing version.

## 3. HERB FILE 004 — MEADOWSWEET (*Filipendula ulmaria*)
The deliberate companion to File 001: the plant aspirin is actually *named* after. Sold as a
pair. It lets the shelf demonstrate that the popular one-plant origin story is itself a
simplification — the strongest possible argument for the shelf's method, told twice.

## 4. HERB FILE SERIES BOUND COLLECTION — Files 001–006
The first six files as one bound edition with a shelf-level introduction on the four
registers, a combined sources section, and a cumulative unknowns index. **$19.00 PROPOSED**,
matching the existing top of the ladder. Requires six files to exist first.

## 5. THE FOUR REGISTERS — a working card deck
Shelf-level method product rather than a plant file: a deck that teaches the reader to sort
any health or history claim they meet into documented / tradition / science / unknown.
Directly reuses the approved Question Deck card architecture and its five-class band coding,
so production cost is low. This is the item that turns the shelf from a series into a method
people can apply — and it is the natural bundle partner for every file.

---

**Shelf note.** Kola (002), cinchona (003) and grains of paradise (held) form a coherent
trade-route sub-series on plants whose origins are routinely under-reported in the mainstream
account. That sub-series is worth naming and sequencing deliberately once File 001 is
approved — it is the shelf's strongest long-run editorial position, and File 001's contested
credit page is what earns the shelf the standing to tell it.
