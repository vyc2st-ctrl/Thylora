# BUILD YOUR GATE
## Eight-page kids' activity and game specification

**Realization:** THY-REALIZE-GATE-BUILDER-KIDS-001
**Route:** BUILD-YOUR-GATE · current gate **OBJECT**
**Age band:** 8–12
**Idea:** Kids learn the THYLORA gate network by building their own "decision gates", which are small checks you pass before doing something. It teaches evidence, permission and second-checking in kid terms. It **does not** use real THYLORA data or real people.
**Price:** $1.49 mini (pages 1–5 and 8) · $4.99 expanded bundle (all 8 pages, plus 24 printable scenario cards and a blank-gate template set). **PROPOSED / NOT LIVE**

|---|---|---|
| PERSON | Who Gate | Who's in it? Did they say yes? |
| PRIVACY | Secret Gate | Is anything private in it? |

| AUTONOMY | Helper Gate | Can a robot helper do this alone? |

### The four signals
- 🟢 **GO** (PASS): everything checked.
- 🟡 **SOME** (PARTIAL): part of it can go; fix the rest.
- 🔴 **WAIT** (HOLD): something is missing. Find it first.
- 🔵 **ASK** (ESCALATE): a grown-up in charge must decide.
Colour is always paired with a word and a shape (circle, half-circle, square, triangle) so colour-blind players are not excluded.

### Page 1 · What is a gate?
A short comic-strip explainer (line art, no real people): a kid wants to post a photo of their friend's birthday cake, and the friend is in the background. The kid "walks through" three gates (Who, Secret, Share) and ends up cropping the friend out *or* asking first.
**Takeaway box:** "A gate is a question you answer *before* you act."

### Page 2 · Parts of a gate
A labelled diagram of one gate with five parts:
1. **The question** (what this gate checks)
2. **The proof** (what you need to show). *Kid version of the evidence floor.*
3. **Who decides** (you, a helper, or a grown-up in charge). *Kid version of the authority floor.*
4. **Double-check** (someone else looks too). *Redundancy check.*
5. **Neighbour gates** (which other gates to tell if this one says WAIT). *Connected gates.*

### Page 3 · Build your first gate (template)
A fill-in gate: *My gate is called ___ · It asks ___ · The proof I need is ___ · Who decides: me / helper / grown-up · My double-checker is ___ · If it says WAIT, I tell these gates: ___*
**Worked example:** the Homework Gate ("Is my homework really done?" Proof: every question answered. Double-checker: a sibling or parent).

### Page 4 · Scenario cards (6 in the mini, 24 in the expanded bundle)
Each card has a situation, the gates to walk through, and a model answer on the back. Mini set:
1. "I want to post a video of my class." → Who, Secret, Share → **ASK** (other kids are in it)
2. "I drew a dragon and want to sell prints at the school fair." → Maker, Ready, Coin, Shop → **ASK** (money, so a grown-up says OK)
3. "My science poster says the sun is a planet." → Fact → **WAIT** (fix the fact)
4. "I found a cool picture online for my report." → Picture, Maker → **SOME** (use it with credit, if the rules allow, or draw your own)
5. "A game app asks for my home address." → Secret, Rule → **ASK**
6. "My robot helper can tidy my room while I'm at school." → Helper → **GO** (inside, safe, nobody else affected)

### Page 5 · Connect the gates (map)
A printed map of all 12 kid gates as circles, with the real adjacency lines from `docs/GATE-NETWORK-620.md`, simplified for 8-year-olds.
**Activity:** trace the path for scenario 2 with a pencil. **Question:** "If the Maker Gate says WAIT, which neighbour gates get a warning?"

### Page 6 · The Gate Game (rules): *expanded bundle*
- **Players:** 2–4. **Materials:** the scenario cards, 12 gate tokens (cut out), 4 signal cards per player.
- **Turn:** draw a scenario, lay down the gate tokens you think it must pass, then show a signal card for each gate.
- **Scoring:** 1 point for each correct gate, 1 point for each correct signal, and **+2 bonus for a correct neighbour warning** (naming a gate that must be told).
- **Skeptic rule:** any player may say "how do you know?" Explaining earns +1.
- **Win:** first to 15. **Co-op variant:** the team must clear 8 cards with no wrong GO signals.

### Page 7 · Design your own gate network: *expanded bundle*
A blank network page. Kids invent 4–6 gates for their own life (a Pet Gate, a Screen-Time Gate), draw the connections, and write one scenario that uses 3 of them.

### Page 8 · Gate Builder certificate and grown-up notes
- A certificate: "___ is a Certified Gate Builder." Includes a date and the signal legend.
- **Grown-up notes (half page):** what the activity teaches (asking before acting, evidence, consent, who decides); how to use the ASK signal at home; a reminder that no personal information is collected by this activity.
