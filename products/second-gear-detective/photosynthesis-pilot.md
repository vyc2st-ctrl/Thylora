### What "Second Gear" means (working definition, confirm)
**First gear** asks *what?* ("Plants need light.")
**Second gear** asks *how do we know?* and *what would happen if…?* ("If plants need light, what would a plant in a closet look like after a week, and why?")
Every card has one first-gear clue and one second-gear question.

### Card 1 · THE CASE OF THE GREEN FACTORY
**Clue:** A tree can weigh as much as a truck, but it never eats. Where does all that stuff come from?
**Detective question:** What is a leaf *for*?
**Evidence:** Leaves are food factories. Inside them, the plant makes its own food (a sugar called glucose) using light, water and a gas from the air. This is called **photosynthesis**: *photo* = light, *synthesis* = putting together.
**Try it:** Collect five different leaves on a walk (only from the ground or with permission). Line them up from biggest to smallest.
**Second gear:** Most of a tree's mass comes from something you cannot see. What could it be? *(Hold the answer until Card 5.)*
**Answer key:** Mostly carbon from carbon dioxide in the air, plus water. Not from the soil.
**Line-art brief:** One leaf drawn as a small factory with a door and a chimney.

### Card 2 · CLUE: THE SUNLIGHT
**Clue:** Plants on a windowsill lean toward the window.
**Detective question:** What are they reaching for?
**Evidence:** Light is the energy source for photosynthesis. Plants grow toward light. This is called *phototropism*.
**Try it:** Put a bean seedling in a shoebox with one hole cut in the side. Check it every day for a week.
**Second gear:** If light gives the energy, what would happen to a plant kept in total darkness for two weeks? What would it *look* like?
**Answer key:** It would grow pale and yellowish, often tall and spindly as it searches for light, and eventually it would run out of stored food.
**Safety:** An adult does any cutting.
**Line-art brief:** A seedling bending toward a hole in a box.

### Card 3 · CLUE: THE WATER
**Clue:** A wilted plant stands back up a few hours after watering.
**Detective question:** How does water get from the soil to the leaves?
**Evidence:** Roots take in water. Tiny tubes (called **xylem**) carry it up the stem to the leaves, like straws. Water is one of the ingredients of photosynthesis.
**Try it:** Put a pale celery stalk with leaves in a cup of water with food colouring. Check it after one day.
**Second gear:** The colour moved *up*. Water usually falls down. What does that tell you about the stem?
**Answer key:** The plant moves water upward. Water evaporating from the leaves pulls more water up the tubes behind it.
**Safety:** Food colouring stains. Use an old cup and cover the table.
**Line-art brief:** A celery stalk with coloured lines running up it.

### Card 4 · CLUE: THE INVISIBLE GAS
**Clue:** Leaves have thousands of tiny doors you cannot see without a microscope.
**Detective question:** What goes in and out of those doors?
**Evidence:** The doors are called **stomata**. Carbon dioxide (CO₂), a gas in the air that you breathe out, goes *in*. Oxygen and water vapour come *out*.
**Try it:** Put a clear plastic bag over a few leaves of a potted plant (not over the soil) and tie it loosely. Look after a few hours in the sun.
**Second gear:** Where did the water drops inside the bag come from, if you did not add any water?
**Answer key:** Water vapour left the leaf through its stomata and condensed on the bag.
**Safety:** Keep plastic bags away from young children's faces.
**Line-art brief:** A magnified leaf surface with mouth-like pores.

### Card 5 · THE SECRET RECIPE
**Clue:** Every factory has a recipe.
**Detective question:** What are the three ingredients, and what are the two things the plant makes?
**Evidence:**
> **carbon dioxide + water —(light energy, in chlorophyll)→ sugar + oxygen**
> 6 CO₂ + 6 H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6 O₂
**Try it:** Draw the recipe as a cooking card, with ingredients, the oven (sunlight) and what comes out.
**Second gear:** Back to Card 1. The tree's mass is mostly carbon. Which ingredient brought the carbon in?
**Answer key:** Carbon dioxide from the air. A tree is built mostly out of air and water.
**Line-art brief:** A recipe card with three ingredient icons and two outputs.

### Card 6 · THE OXYGEN ALIBI
**Clue:** Pondweed in sunlight makes tiny bubbles.
**Detective question:** What is in the bubbles?
**Evidence:** The bubbles are mostly oxygen, a *by-product* of photosynthesis. The oxygen comes from splitting **water**, not from the carbon dioxide. Much of the oxygen in the air was made by plants and algae.
**Try it (with an adult):** Put a sprig of pondweed (for example, elodea from an aquarium shop) in a clear jar of water in bright light. Count the bubbles in one minute. Move the jar into shade and count again.
**Second gear:** Why would the bubble count change when you moved the jar into the shade?
**Answer key:** Less light means less photosynthesis, so less oxygen is made.
**Safety:** Adult supervision near water. Do not use pond water from the wild; buy the plant.
**Line-art brief:** A jar with a green sprig and small bubbles rising.

### Card 7 · THE SUGAR STASH
**Clue:** A potato is a plant's lunchbox.
**Detective question:** What does a plant do with the sugar it makes?
**Evidence:** The plant uses sugar for energy right away. It stores extra as **starch** (in potatoes, seeds and roots) and builds its walls from **cellulose**, which is also made from sugar.
**Try it (with an adult):** Put one drop of iodine solution on a slice of raw potato and one drop on a slice of apple. Compare the colours.
**Second gear:** The potato turned blue-black and the apple mostly did not. What does that tell you about where plants store food?
**Answer key:** Blue-black means starch. Potatoes are storage organs full of starch.
**Safety:** Iodine is **poisonous if swallowed** and it stains. **An adult handles it.** Throw the slices away afterward and **do not eat them**. Wash hands.
**Line-art brief:** A potato slice with a dark spot and an apple slice without one.

### Card 8 · WHY ARE LEAVES GREEN?
**Clue:** Sunlight looks white, but it is really made of all the colours.
**Detective question:** If leaves use light, why do they look green?
**Evidence:** **Chlorophyll** is the green pigment in leaves. It absorbs mostly red and blue light and uses that energy. It reflects green light, so green is the colour that reaches our eyes.
**Try it (with an adult):** Put a crushed leaf in a little rubbing alcohol in a cup, then stand a coffee-filter strip in it for about an hour. Look for bands of colour.
**Second gear:** In autumn some leaves turn yellow and orange. Were those colours there all along?
**Answer key:** Often yes. Yellow and orange pigments (carotenoids) are hidden under the green. When chlorophyll breaks down in autumn, they show.
**Safety:** Rubbing alcohol is flammable and should not be swallowed. An adult pours it. Keep it away from flames. Use good ventilation.
**Line-art brief:** A prism splitting light onto a leaf; green arrows bounce off.

### Card 9 · THE NIGHT SHIFT
**Clue:** At night there is no sunlight.
**Detective question:** Do plants stop working at night?
**Evidence:** Photosynthesis needs light, so it stops in the dark. But plants **respire** (use sugar for energy and release carbon dioxide) all the time, day and night, just like animals.
**Try it:** Make a two-column chart, **DAY** and **NIGHT**, and write which jobs a plant does in each.
**Second gear:** If plants respire day and night but photosynthesize only in the day, how can they still end up with extra food?
**Answer key:** In daylight they make more sugar than they use, so the extra is stored for the night.
**Line-art brief:** A plant under a sun on the left and under a moon on the right.

### Card 10 · CASE CLOSED
**Clue:** You had breakfast this morning.
**Detective question:** Can you trace your breakfast back to a leaf?
**Evidence:** Almost every food chain on land starts with photosynthesis. Cereal comes from grain, and grain is a plant. Milk comes from a cow, and a cow eats grass. The energy in your food was once sunlight captured by a leaf.
**Try it:** Pick one food you ate today. Draw arrows back, step by step, until you reach a plant and the sun.
**Second gear:** Name one living thing that does *not* need energy from photosynthesis. *(Hint: think deep ocean.)*
**Answer key:** Some deep-sea vent communities get energy from chemicals (chemosynthesis) instead of sunlight.
**Detective badge:** "I traced my food back to the sun." Fill in: name, date, the food, the chain.
**Line-art brief:** A breakfast bowl with a dotted trail leading back to a wheat stalk and the sun.
