It is still bound to the product. **I did not unbind it.** The product is DRAFT, so no customer can
see it, and removing it would destroy the record of what was bound. Unbinding is a Chairman action
taken together with approving the replacement, not a cleanup done quietly beforehand.

## What the cover has to do

This is a paired digital sheet: a trail-cook story that becomes a kitchen card. The cover's job is to
tell a buyer, in one glance, that they are getting **both** — a story worth reading and a recipe
worth cooking. A photograph of beans says recipe. A frontier illustration says story. The typographic
route says both, which is why the realization record chose it.

## The brief

**Format:** 1:1, matching the other eight store covers. Typographic. No photograph, no illustration,
no AI-generated scene.

**Text, exactly:**

```
TRAIL TABLE
No. 001

UNKLE SEEZIN'S
RAIN-SIDE BEANS

A trail-cook story and a kitchen card
```

**Canon rules binding this cover:**

- **Unkle Seezin** is the subject. Spelled that way. The product's own description carries the
  ruling: *"Old Gerald is a separate person and must not be substituted for Unkle Seezin."*
- The historical source title — *Old Jaro's Rain-Side Beans & Quick Sourdough Biscuits* — is
  **provenance, not cover text.** It stays in the realization record where it belongs. It must not
  appear on the cover, and it is not an error to be scrubbed from the record either.
- **EdereAriah**, not "EdereAirah". The incumbent image's alt text has the letters transposed. Do not
  inherit that spelling.

**Treatment direction:** the two other Unkle Seezin products set the house voice — plain, weathered,
unhurried. Type should sit like something stencilled on a supply crate rather than set for a
restaurant menu. No rain effects, no dripping type, no literal illustration of weather. The word
"Rain-Side" is doing that work already.

**What must not happen:** no generic food stock, no generic western stock, no image that could be
swapped onto another product without anyone noticing. A cover that would work for any recipe is not a
product-specific visual, and product-specific visual is the gate.
