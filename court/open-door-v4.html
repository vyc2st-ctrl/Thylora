We built a clearer courtroom record.
We would like to show you how.

Who we are

THYLORA is a company that builds a complete simulated world. That world has its own government, its own courts, its own court officers and its own records. We run real courtroom workflows inside it so we can find the places where a record breaks before the same break happens in a real courtroom.

We are writing to you as a counterpart. Your office runs a real court. We run a simulated one. The problems are the same: a ruling is spoken, a clerk has to capture it, and every person who reads the record later has to understand it the same way.

How to read this packet

Every item in this packet comes from one of two places, and the frame around it tells you which:

EARTH SOURCE

Black frame, no seal. This material comes from the real world: a real court, a real document or a real person. We quote it. We do not change it.

THYLORA SIMULATION

Red frame, red seal. This material comes from our simulated world. The people named inside a red box are officers of our world, not of any Earth government. The red seal is our world's seal. It is not a seal of the State of Maryland or of any Earth government.

Why we write this way

When a word can be read two ways, people pick one. The next person inherits that reading and passes it on. After three handoffs, the office is running on a guess that nobody remembers making. We call this interpretation drift.

So we do not aim for simple. We aim for clear and complete: every time, every date, every person and every "who tells whom" is written out, so the record explains itself and everyone who reads it gets the same meaning the first time.

What we are asking

30 minutes with your courtroom clerks. We will take one real entry from your court, with names removed, and rewrite it with them using the method in this packet. If it does not save time or prevent a single question, you have lost 30 minutes. Contact: ADD CONTACT

Part 2 · What this office can try on Monday before court

One clerk entry, rewritten so it explains itself

The left side shows a typical entry. Each phrase on the left can be read more than one way. The right side shows the same entry with every possible reading closed off.

Typical entry (can be read many ways)

Recess. Back in 10 minutes. Will update if anything changes. Disposition: per bench.

  • "Recess": for what purpose? Who called it?
  • "Back in 10 minutes": 10 minutes from when? Is the recess 10 minutes long, or does court return after the 10th minute?
  • "Will update": who updates, and whom do they tell?
  • "Per bench": what exactly was ordered?

Clear entry (one reading only)

Recess called byThe presiding judge
PurposeThe judge is reviewing the exhibit submitted by defense counsel
Recess began10:30 AM (clerk reads courtroom clock)
Stated return time10:40 AM, stated by the judge on the record
If the return time changesThe courtroom clerk announces the new clock time in the courtroom to counsel and to the parties present, and records the new time and the reason
DispositionWritten out in full: sentence, conditions, date, and form numbers

The rule behind it: what the clerk captures, every time

FieldWhat "clear" means for this field
The rulingThe words of the ruling as spoken, and who spoke them.
PurposeOne concrete reason in plain words ("to review Exhibit 4"). "Purpose" means different things to different people, so the clerk writes the reason itself, not a label.
TimesClock times (10:40 AM), never durations alone ("10 minutes"). If a duration is spoken, the clerk records both: "10 minutes, return at 10:40 AM".
Who tells whomEvery update names the person giving it and the people receiving it.
Required dispositionSentence, conditions, effective date and form numbers, each on its own line.

How we teach ambiguity without being ambiguous

We show trainees an unclear entry, ask them to write down what they think it means, and compare their answers. The answers always differ. Then we show the clear version and the single direction we want. The lesson is not "be careful". The lesson is "write it so there is only one way to read it."

Part 3 · The measurements, written the way they would appear on a classroom board

Each measurement is shown four ways: the expression by itself, a key to every letter, the question the expression asks, and a worked example.

1 · How to measure change without turning people into scores
ΔQ = Qafter − Qbefore
Δ"Delta", the Greek letter for change. QThe quantity measured: one process number, never a person's score. Example: minutes from a spoken ruling to the finished clerk entry. QbeforeThe average of that number over the 20 court days before the new method starts. QafterThe average of the same number, measured the same way, over the 20 court days after the new method starts.
The question this expression asksHow far did the measured quantity move between the 20 court days before the change and the 20 court days after it?
Example: ΔQ = 6 − 14 = −8. Entries finished 8 minutes faster. For a time measurement, a negative ΔQ means improvement.
2 · Is the record worth relying on?
PV = A × T × U × R
PVPractical value of one record, from 0 (worthless) to 1 (fully reliable). AAccurate: matches what was said and ordered (0 to 1). TTimely: finished within the time the office requires (0 to 1). UUsable: the next person can act on it without asking (0 to 1). RRelied on: the people who receive it trust it enough to act on it (0 to 1).
The question this expression asksIs the record accurate, timely, usable and trusted, all at the same time?
Why multiply instead of add: a record that is perfect in three ways and inaccurate in one is not 75% useful. If any factor is 0, PV is 0. Example: 1 × 1 × 1 × 0 = 0.
3 · Why the same letter Y sometimes carries a t and sometimes an x
Yt   and   Yx
YThe outcome being measured, for example entries finished the same day. tAnswers when. Yt is the outcome on day t (Y1 is day 1, Y2 is day 2). xAnswers under which condition. Yx is the outcome when condition x is used (x = old entry form, or x = new entry form).
The question this notation asksAre we comparing the same thing across time (t), or across conditions (x)? Mixing the two makes it look as if a change caused a result when time alone did.

4 · The court relationship as one measurement
C = H × Q × K
CCourt quality: how well one handoff in the courtroom works (0 to 1). In our notes, a capital C always means court. HHandoff: the information reached the next person whole (0 to 1). QQuestion quality: the questions asked closed the gaps instead of opening new ones (0 to 1). KClarity: the record has exactly one reading (0 to 1).
The question this expression asksWhen information moves from the bench to the clerk to the next office, does it arrive complete, answered and with only one meaning?

Survey-ready answers: what that means

A survey-ready answer is an answer written so that a clerk who takes the same survey could repeat it word for word and the coordinators could count it without interpreting it. Each answer names what was observed, who observed it and when.

Every survey question matters. Earlier versions of this packet featured two questions (numbers 3 and 9). That made those two look like the only ones that counted. They were featured because they concern timing and handoffs, which this packet measures. The full set of questions is answered in the attached sheet ATTACH SHEET.

One more idea: decide the facts before seeing the faces

A decision about what happened should not depend on who the people are. In our simulated courts we test a two-pass review:

  1. Pass one: facts only. The reviewer sees "Person 1" and "Person 2". Names, faces, skin color, neighborhood and vehicle are removed. The reviewer decides who acted first and what the evidence shows.
  2. Pass two: context. Identity and circumstances are restored, for example fear for one's life or why something was taken. Context shapes understanding and compassion. It does not reverse a fact finding made on the evidence.

This idea already exists on Earth. California Penal Code section 741 (from Assembly Bill 2778, signed in 2022) requires prosecutors to do an initial race-blind charging review, starting 1 January 2025. Offices that adopted it reported real costs for redaction software and staff. We would study that cost before recommending the same step anywhere.
