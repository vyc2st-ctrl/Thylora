QYRIS Quickcheck

Five questions you answer before you commit — and the one rule that makes them bite.

Most bad decisions are not made by people who skipped thinking. They are made by people who thought hard about the wrong five minutes. QYRIS is a fixed five-field inspection that forces the thinking into the part that actually decides the outcome, and then refuses to let you mark the thing done until each field is answered.

It takes about eight minutes. It is designed to be run before money, a public claim, a release, or an irreversible step — not after.

The rule that makes it work No active, ready, or done state without a QYRIS pass. Not "we discussed it." A written answer in all five fields, or the thing is on hold. A gate you can talk your way past is not a gate.

The five fields

Q · Question

What is actually being asked?

Write the request in the words it arrived in — then write what it is really asking for.

Most failures start here. Someone asks for a report; what they need is a decision. Someone asks for a faster build; what they need is to stop shipping broken ones. If those two sentences differ, everything downstream is aimed at the wrong target, and no amount of execution quality fixes it.

Done when: you have written both sentences and they are different sentences, or you can say why they are the same.

Y · Yield

What does this produce, in plain words?

If this goes exactly as planned, what exists afterward that does not exist now?

Name the artifact, the state change, or the money. "Improved alignment" is not a yield. "A signed contract," "a working checkout," "four hours a week back" are yields. If you cannot name the thing, you cannot later tell whether you got it — and work that cannot be checked tends to be reported as complete.

Done when: a stranger could look at the world afterward and tell you whether it happened.

R · Reason

Why does this matter enough to do now?

What gets worse if this waits a month? What gets worse if it never happens?

This field kills more work than the other four combined, which is its job. Much of what fills a week is real, legitimate, useful — and not load-bearing. Reason separates the two. If nothing gets worse when it waits, it waits, and something that does get worse moves up.

Done when: you have named a specific cost of delay, or you have decided to defer it on purpose.

I · Inspect

Known, unknown, next step.

Three lists. What you can evidence. What you cannot. The one thing you do next.

This is the field people fake, by moving items from "unknown" to "known" on the strength of a strong feeling. The discipline is simple and hard: an item is known only if you can point at where it is written. Everything else is unknown — including things you are confident about.

A long unknown list is not a failure. An empty unknown list on a decision of any size is almost always a lie you are telling yourself.

Done when: every "known" has a pointer, and the next step is one action, not a plan.

S · Safeguard

What protects the people this touches if it goes wrong?

Who is exposed, how, and what catches them.

Not risk to the project — risk to people. The customer who paid, the person whose name is on it, the one who inherits the mess. Then ask the harder version: is the safeguard a mechanism, or an intention? "We'll be careful" is an intention. "The database refuses the write" is a mechanism. Intentions fail quietly on the day everyone is busy.

Done when: each exposed party has a mechanism, or you have written down that they do not.

How to score it

QYRIS does not average. A check is PASS only when all five fields are answered. Any field blank is HOLD, and hold means the work does not move to active, ready, or done — regardless of how far along it already is.

StateMeansWhat you do
PASSAll five answered, Inspect has pointersProceed
HOLDA field is blank, or a "known" has no pointerFill it, or stop
HOLDActive work with no check on record at allRun the check before the next step
The third row is the one that matters Work already in flight with no check is not "fine because it's going well." It is unchecked. Absence of a check is a hold, not a pass — otherwise the gate only ever catches the careful.

Four failures this catches

1 · The confident unknown

Someone lists a dependency as known. Asked where it says so, nobody can point. Inspect converts confidence into a pointer or into an unknown — there is no third option.

2 · The answered-but-not-asked

The work is excellent and delivers something nobody asked for. Question catches it in the first two minutes, before the excellent work happens.

3 · The urgent non-essential

Something loud crowds out something load-bearing. Reason asks what gets worse if it waits, and loud things frequently have no answer.

4 · The intention dressed as a control

"We'll review that carefully." Safeguard asks whether the protection is a mechanism or a plan to remember. On the bad day, only mechanisms are still running.

The worksheet

One check, one page. Print it, or copy the headings.

A worked example

SubjectTurn on recurring subscription billing this week
QAsked: "switch on subscriptions." Actually asking: can we take money monthly and have access match what was paid for, including when someone cancels.
YA member pays, gains the right channels, cancels, and loses them — proven once end to end on a real account.
REvery month without it is a month of members who would have paid and could not. Cost of delay is countable, so it is real.
IKnown: prices and channel entitlements are approved — the store page states them. Unknown: whether cancellation actually removes access; no one has run it. Next step: run one controlled subscribe → pay → access → cancel → access-removed cycle.
SExposed: anyone charged for access they do not get, or who cancels and is charged again. Mechanism: no purchase button until the cycle passes; no live-commerce claim on the page meanwhile.
StateHOLD — one unknown blocks it, and the next step is a single action.

Note what the example does not do: it does not call the work blocked and stop. It names one unknown and one action that removes it. A good QYRIS hold is a short hold.

Where this comes from

QYRIS is the inspection THYLORA runs on its own work. The five fields, the pass rule, and the "active with no check is a hold" rule are the same ones enforced in the THYLORA backend, where a check is a stored record with five fields rather than a conversation. This quickcheck is that method, extracted so it can be run with a pen.
